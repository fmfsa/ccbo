\begin{abstract}
Causal Bayesian optimization (\CBO{}) accelerates the search for optimal
interventions by using a causal graph both to build informative priors and to
prune the space of candidate interventions. This leverage rests on a brittle
assumption: the entire DAG must be specified correctly, and a recent survey of
the field
names robustness to structural misspecification a central open problem
\citep{anonymous2026cbobench}. We first make precise how a single wrong edge
enters the algorithm: it biases the do-calculus \emph{prior}, it can change the
\emph{search set} itself by flipping an identifiability verdict, and --- in
model-based methods --- it corrupts the \emph{surrogate's architecture}. We
then propose coarsening as an \emph{input contract}: the practitioner supplies
only a partition of the manipulable variables and the cluster-level (quotient)
graph, and we prove that \emph{any} optimizer whose graph-dependent components
factor through this quotient is invariant --- exploration sets, priors,
surrogates, and the entire trajectory --- to every misspecification confined
within a cluster. We instantiate the contract twice: \QCBO{}, arm-based
quotient \CBO{} with complete identification on the C-DAG; and \QMCBO{},
which coarsens model-based \CBO{}'s mechanism factorization into joint
per-cluster mechanisms. Both are byte-for-byte invariant under within-cluster
edge errors that measurably shift their full-DAG counterparts, and coarsening
additionally concentrates the intervention budget. The price is resolution:
when the optimum splits a cluster, a fixed partition pays a linear-in-$T$
regret, which a plateau-triggered refinement down a declared hierarchy turns
into a bounded transient. We prove this trade is intrinsic: evaluating
sub-cluster interventions is not identifiable from quotient-level inputs
alone, in either family --- resolution below the committed partition must be
bought with intra-cluster structure, i.e.\ with exposure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Causal Bayesian optimization \citep{aglietti2020causal} unifies two ideas: when
the variable $Y$ we wish to optimize sits inside a causal system, the right
search space is the space of \emph{interventions} $\doo(\mathbf{X}_s=\mathbf{x}_s)$, and
observational data plus the causal graph yield informative priors over the
interventional response surfaces. The catch is the input requirement. \CBO{} and
its descendants \citep{aglietti2021dynamic,aglietti2023constrained,sussex2023model}
assume the full DAG is specified correctly. The recent survey of \citet{anonymous2026cbobench}
organizes the field along design axes, of which \emph{graph knowledge} is the
first, and lists robustness to structural error among the open problems the field
has yet to address. Our thesis is that this fragility can be better accounted
for by committing to \emph{less} structure of
a more reliable kind.

\paragraph{A wrong edge is costly.}
The assumed graph enters a \CBO{} algorithm only through its \emph{derived
causal-effect estimators}, and corrupting those is consequential in two ways.
(i) Through the \emph{prior}: the do-calculus estimate used as the GP prior mean
is a functional of the graph, so a wrong edge biases it even when the intervention
is reachable and the true effect is unchanged --- a convergence-speed cost paid in
GAP and PA-GAP. (ii) Through the \emph{exploration set}: the candidate arms are
themselves derived from the graph --- pruned to the (possibly-optimal) minimal
intervention sets of structural causal bandits \citep{lee2018structural} and, in
do-calculus-prior pipelines such as ours, additionally gated on identifiability
--- so a misspecified edge can flip a verdict and
\emph{remove} the candidate containing the optimum.
Either way the damage is a functional of the wrong edge: an estimator that
\emph{cannot see} an edge cannot be damaged by
it. Remark~\ref{rem:taxonomy} makes the dichotomy precise, and the evaluation of
\S\ref{sec:exp} is organized around it.

\paragraph{Coarser commitments decide less, so less can be wrong.}
A cluster-level structural belief encodes strictly fewer decisions than a full
DAG: one aggregate judgement between two groups of variables stands in for a
bundle of edge-level judgements the modeler is never asked to make --- and a
commitment that decides less is less likely to be incorrect. Both ends of this
spectrum are already served: full-DAG
\CBO{} at one end, and methods that learn structure online at heavy per-trial
cost at the other \citep{mukherjee2024gacbo,branchini2023ceo}. The practical
middle --- reliable cluster-level structure --- has a developed identification
theory \citep{anand2023cdag,ferreira2025cdmg,wahl2024foundations} but, to our
knowledge, no optimization method.

\paragraph{Quotient \CBO{}.}
\QCBO{} fills this gap. The practitioner supplies the cluster-level structure
directly: a partition $\Pi$ of the manipulable variables together with the
C-DAG $\Gq$ over its clusters (when a fine-grained hypothesis graph is
available, $\Gq$ is its quotient, with bidirected edges absorbing latent
confounding via the latent projection of \citealp{lee2019latent}). \QCBO{}
restricts exploration to cluster-level
interventions whose effects are identifiable from $\Gq$ and uses the identified
cluster-level do-calculus estimates as GP prior means. The central structural
insight is that this input \emph{never looks inside a cluster}: an edge
error confined to a cluster cannot be represented, let alone affect the optimizer.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Robustness as an input contract.} We prove that quotient
  invariance is \emph{method-agnostic} (Theorem~\ref{thm:contract}): any
  optimizer whose graph-dependent components factor through $(\Pi,\Gq)$ is
  invariant to arbitrary within-cluster edge errors. We instantiate the
  contract twice --- arm-based (\QCBO{}, Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}) and
  model-based (\QMCBO{}, \S\ref{sec:qmcbo}), the latter coarsening MCBO's
  mechanism factorization, the method class most exposed to a wrong graph
  because the DAG is its surrogate's architecture. Both are demonstrated
  byte-identical under within-cluster errors that measurably shift their
  full-DAG counterparts, and the scope is \emph{exact}: the same edge placed
  across clusters breaks the guarantee (\S\ref{sec:exp}).
  \item \textbf{Sample efficiency.} Coarsening concentrates a fixed
  budget on fewer identifiable cluster-level arms; a price-of-coarsening
  characterization (Prop.~\ref{prop:price}) gives an exact condition for when this
  costs nothing. On the benchmark suite the \textsc{CausalBO} survey aggregates
  from the CBO literature,
  under its common GAP/PA-GAP scoring protocol, \QCBO{}-coarse attains higher
  GAP than full-knowledge \CBO{} at comparable final $Y$. When the partition
  \emph{is} lossy, a plateau-triggered walk down a declared hierarchy
  (\S\ref{sec:refine}) converts the price into a bounded transient: fixed-coarse
  pays cumulative regret linear in $T$; hierarchical \QCBO{} does not.
  \item \textbf{The exposure principle.} Sub-cluster interventions are
  provably \emph{not identifiable} from quotient-level inputs
  (Prop.~\ref{prop:qexposure}): in both families, resolution below the
  committed partition must be bought with intra-cluster structure or finer
  interventional data. Refinement's phase-scoped invariance
  (\S\ref{sec:refine}) and \QMCBO{}'s soundness at cluster granularity
  (Prop.~\ref{prop:qsound}) delimit exactly what the purchase costs and what
  survives it.
  \item \textbf{Methods and theory.} We formalize \QCBO{} --- complete
  identification on the C-DAG \citep{shpitser2006identification}, an
  exploration set restricted to identifiable cluster unions, a two-tier causal
  GP prior (\S\ref{sec:method}) --- and \QMCBO{} (\S\ref{sec:qmcbo});
  supporting theory is in \S\ref{sec:theory}, with proofs in
  App.~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

Random variables are upper case, values lower case, and vectors and sets of
variables bold; optimization-layer notation follows \citet{aglietti2020causal}
and partition/coarsening notation follows \citet{madaleno2026coarsening}, with
each symbol introduced where first used.

\paragraph{SCMs and interventions.}
A structural causal model $\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V},F,
P(\mathbf{U})\rangle$ induces a distribution over observables $\mathbf{V}$ and,
for any $\mathbf{X}_s\subseteq\mathbf{V}$, interventional distributions
$P(\cdot\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s=
\mathbf{x}_s))$ obtained by replacing the mechanisms of $\mathbf{X}_s$ with constants
\citep{pearl2009causality}. The induced graph $\Gg$ has a directed edge per
functional argument and a bidirected edge per shared latent; projecting out
latents yields an ADMG over $\mathbf{V}$ \citep{lee2019latent}.

\paragraph{Causal Bayesian optimization.}
Given manipulable variables $\mathbf{X}\subseteq\mathbf{V}$ and target $Y$,
\CBO{} \citep{aglietti2020causal} solves
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:cbo_obj}
  (\mathbf{X}_s^\star,\mathbf{x}_s^\star) =
  \arg\min_{\substack{\mathbf{X}_s\in\ES\\ \mathbf{x}_s\in D(\mathbf{X}_s)}}
  \E\!\left[Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s=\mathbf{x}_s)\right],
\end{equation}
where $D(\mathbf{X}_s)$ is the interventional domain of $\mathbf{X}_s$,
maintaining one GP per \emph{candidate intervention set} $\mathbf{X}_s$ in an
exploration set $\ES$, with prior mean given by the do-calculus estimate of
$\E[Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s=\mathbf{x}_s)]$ from observational data and the assumed graph,
and selecting $(\mathbf{X}_s,\mathbf{x}_s)$ by expected improvement
\citep{garnett2023bayesian}. Each candidate is itself a \emph{continuous} BO
sub-problem over $\mathbf{x}_s$. The exploration set is pruned structurally
(MIS/POMIS; \citealp{lee2018structural}); both the pruning and the priors are
functions of the assumed graph, which is where misspecification enters.

\paragraph{Coarsening and C-DAGs.}
\begin{definition}[Coarsening~\citep{madaleno2026coarsening}]
\label{def:coarsening}
Given a DAG $\Gg$ on $\mathbf{V}$, a partition $\Pi=\{C_1,\ldots,C_K\}$ of
$\mathbf{V}$ induces the surjection $\chi:\mathbf{V}\to\Pi$ mapping each vertex
to its cluster, and the quotient graph $\Gq$ with vertex set $\Pi$ and edge set
$\{\chi(V_i)\to\chi(V_j) \mid V_i\to V_j\in\Gg,\ \chi(V_i)\neq\chi(V_j)\}$. If
$\Gq$ is acyclic, it is a \textbf{coarsening} of $\Gg$ and $\Pi$ is called
\emph{valid}.
\end{definition}
For ADMGs the quotient additionally carries $C_k\leftrightarrow C_\ell$ iff some
$V_i\in C_k,V_j\in C_\ell$ are connected by a bidirected edge; intra-cluster
bidirected edges are dropped. The resulting object is a C-DAG in the sense of
\citet{anand2023cdag}, who prove that do-calculus on the C-DAG is sound and
complete for the equivalence class of fine-grained graphs that quotient to it.
Markov properties transfer to the cluster level, but faithfulness does not
\citep{wahl2024foundations}.

\section{The first instantiation: Quotient \CBO{} (\QCBO{})}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\QCBO{} consumes only cluster-level structure: a partition $\Pi$ of the
manipulable variables $\mathbf{X}$ and a C-DAG $\Gq$ over its clusters and the
atomic vertices. The target $Y$ is kept as a singleton, and every non-manipulable
observable remains an atomic (singleton) vertex. Mixing
manipulable and non-manipulable variables in one cluster is disallowed ---
intervening on a cluster must be a physically executable action. When the
practitioner instead holds a fine-grained hypothesis graph $\Gg$, $\Gq$ is
obtained by projecting out hidden
confounders \citep{lee2019latent}, giving an
ADMG $\Gg^{*}$ over observables, and quotienting $\Gg^{*}$ under $\Pi$; \QCBO{}
itself never requires the fine graph. Intervening on a cluster $C_k$ means jointly setting
all of its members, $\doo(C_k=\mathbf{c}_k)$. The candidate exploration set
contains all unions of at most $m$ clusters:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ccbo_exploration}
  \ESpi_{0} = \Bigl\{\,\bigcup_{C_k\in\mathcal{S}}C_k \;\Big|\;
  \emptyset\neq\mathcal{S}\subseteq\Pi,\;|\mathcal{S}|\leq m\,\Bigr\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Intervention granularity]
\label{rem:granularity}
Merging manipulable variables coarsens the \emph{action space}: \QCBO{} can only
set a cluster jointly, never a strict subset of it. This is the mechanism behind
both the robustness (\S\ref{sec:theory}) and the price (Prop.~\ref{prop:price}) of
coarsening.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Identifiable exploration set and the two-tier prior}
\label{sec:causal_prior}
For each candidate $\mathbf{X}_s\in\ESpi_0$ we pose the cluster-level query
$P(Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s))$
on $\Gq$ and run the complete ID algorithm of
\citet{shpitser2006identification} (via \texttt{ananke};
\citealp{lee2023ananke}). Following the arm-pruning tradition of structural
causal bandits \citep{lee2018structural}, \QCBO{} \emph{commits to its structural
beliefs}: the exploration set keeps exactly the candidates whose effect is
identifiable from $\Gq$,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:es_gate}
  \ESpi = \{\,\mathbf{X}_s\in\ESpi_0 \,:\, P(Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s))\text{ id.\ from }\Gq\,\}.
\end{equation}
This restriction is exact (ID is complete on the C-DAG, and identification from $\Gq$ is
sound for every fine graph in its class; \citealp{anand2023cdag}), and it is what
transmits structural misspecification into behavior: a wrong assumed graph keeps
or removes \emph{arms}, not just priors (\S\ref{sec:exp}). The alternative design
--- exploring non-identified arms under an uninformative prior --- trades this
structural damage for exploration overhead; we discuss it in
\S\ref{sec:limitations}.

For $\mathbf{X}_s\in\ESpi$, the GP prior mean is constructed in two tiers. Identifiability
guarantees a non-parametric formula exists; our \emph{estimator} instantiates it
as a cascade of GP-based plug-ins (backdoor adjustment $\to$ front-door $\to$
$g$-computation) on the bidirected-edge expansion of $\Gq$. The cascade is sound
but not complete, so on the rare queries it cannot instantiate we fall back to the
observational mean (each fallback is logged as a diagnostic):
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:two_tier_prior}
  m_s(\mathbf{x}_s) = \begin{cases}
    \hat{\E}[Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s=\mathbf{x}_s)] & \text{cascade succeeds,}\\
    \bar{Y}_{\mathrm{obs}} & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Because clusters are manipulable-only and interventions assign full clusters, the
cluster-level formulas expand exactly into fine-grained regressions on
observational data.

\begin{assumption}[Valid C-DAG]
\label{ass:valid}
The directed part of the assumed cluster-level structure $\Gq$ --- over the
clusters of $\Pi$ and the atomic vertices --- is acyclic. The requirement
constrains only \emph{inter}-cluster structure; intra-cluster structure is
unconstrained, and fine-level acyclicity within clusters can even be relaxed in
some settings \citep{madaleno2026cycles}. When $\Gq$ is obtained by quotienting
a fine hypothesis graph, the assumption is exactly validity of the coarsening
(Def.~\ref{def:coarsening}); either way it is checkable directly on the
cluster-level object and is verified by the implementation.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Estimator-completeness boundary]
\label{ass:id_scheme}
The ID test (Eq.~\ref{eq:es_gate}) is complete; the GP estimator cascade behind
Eq.~\eqref{eq:two_tier_prior} is sound but not complete. Queries that are
identifiable but miss the cascade are reported and receive the uninformative tier. In all
experiments of \S\ref{sec:exp} this set is empty.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Algorithm}
\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\QCBO{}: Quotient Causal Bayesian Optimization}
\label{alg:ccbo}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE C-DAG $\Gq$ over the clusters of $\Pi$ (or a fine hypothesis $\Gg$ and
$\Pi$); target $Y$; budget $T$; observational data $\mathcal{D}^{O}$; cluster
budget $m$; optional hierarchy $H$ (\S\ref{sec:refine}).
\STATE If given $\Gg$: $\Gg^*\leftarrow$ latent projection of $\Gg$;\quad
$\Gq\leftarrow$ quotient of $\Gg^*$ under $\Pi$. Check acyclicity of $\Gq$
(Assumption~\ref{ass:valid}).
\STATE Build $\ESpi$ via Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ccbo_exploration},\eqref{eq:es_gate} (one
complete-ID call per candidate).
\STATE For each $\mathbf{X}_s\in\ESpi$: fit the prior mean $m_s$ via
Eq.~\eqref{eq:two_tier_prior} on $\mathcal{D}^{O}$.
\STATE Collect initial interventional data $\mathcal{D}^{I}_0$.
\FOR{$t=1,\ldots,T$}
  \STATE Update GP posteriors $(m_{s,t},k_{s,t})$ for all $\mathbf{X}_s\in\ESpi$.
  \STATE $(\mathbf{X}_{s_t},\mathbf{x}_t)\leftarrow\arg\max_{s,\mathbf{x}}
  \mathrm{EI}^{s}_{t}(\mathbf{x})/Co(\mathbf{X}_s,\mathbf{x})$.
  \STATE Intervene, observe $y_t$; update $\mathcal{D}^{I}_t$.
  \STATE \textbf{if} $H$ declared \textbf{and} incumbent plateaued: split the
  incumbent's clusters via $H$, re-check validity, rebuild
  $\ESpi\,\cup$ past arms, carry $\mathcal{D}^{I}_t$ by arm, init new arms
  (\S\ref{sec:refine}).
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $(\mathbf{X}_{s_{t^\star}},\mathbf{x}_{t^\star})$ where $t^\star=\arg\min_t y_t$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{The quotient contract: what coarsening buys}
\label{sec:theory}
These results are a \emph{modeling} contribution rather than new estimation
theory: \QCBO{}'s inputs are the quotient $\Gq$ and data, and the quotient is by
construction blind to intra-cluster edges.
Props.~\ref{prop:refinement_validity}--\ref{prop:price} relate quotients of a
common underlying graph (in practice, the true one); \QCBO{} itself consumes
only $\Gq$ (Assumption~\ref{ass:valid}).
Throughout, $\Pi,\Pi'$ are manipulable-only partitions;
$\Pi_\bot$ is the finest (all-singleton) partition, the bottom of the partition
refinement lattice \citep{madaleno2026coarsening}; $\Pi'\preceq\Pi$ ($\Pi'$
\emph{refines} $\Pi$) iff every cluster of $\Pi'$ is contained in a cluster of
$\Pi$.

\begin{proposition}[Identity recovery]
\label{prop:identity}
Under $\Pi=\Pi_\bot$, $\Gq=\Gg^{*}$ and $\ESpi_0$ is the set of all subsets of
$\mathbf{X}$ of size at most $m$; \QCBO{} reduces to full-DAG \CBO{}, with the
exploration set restricted to the identifiable subsets.
\end{proposition}

The invariance guarantee is not specific to \QCBO{}: it is a property of the
\emph{input contract}, holding for any optimizer that consumes the structural
hypothesis only through its quotient. We state it once, method-agnostically;
\QCBO{} (Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}) and the model-based instantiation of
\S\ref{sec:qmcbo} (Cor.~\ref{cor:qmcbo_invariance}) inherit it with their own
byte-identity demonstrations.

\begin{theorem}[Quotient invariance is method-agnostic]
\label{thm:contract}
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be any causal Bayesian optimizer each of whose
graph-dependent components --- search space, identification formulas, priors,
surrogate architecture, and selection rules --- depends on the structural
hypothesis $\Gg$ only through the pair $(\Pi, \Gq)$, where $\Gq$ is the
quotient of $\Gg$'s latent projection under a valid $\Pi$. Then for any two
acyclic hypotheses $\Gg,\Gg'$ with $(\Gg')^{\Pi}=\Gq$, and the same data,
seeds, and hyperparameters, $\mathcal{A}$ produces identical trajectories
under $\Gg$ and $\Gg'$. In particular, this holds for every edit (insertion,
deletion, or reversal of directed or bidirected edges) confined within a
single cluster, and for inter-cluster edits that are redundant in the
quotient. (Proof: App.~\ref{app:proofs} --- every decision of $\mathcal{A}$ at
trial $t$ is a function of $(\Pi,\Gq)$, the data collected so far, and the RNG
state; induction over trials.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Invariance to intra-cluster misspecification]
\label{prop:invariance}
Let $\Pi$ be valid for $\Gg$ and let $\Gg'$ differ from $\Gg$ by an arbitrary set
of insertions, deletions, or reversals of edges (directed or bidirected) whose
endpoints lie within a single cluster of $\Pi$, with $\Gg'$ acyclic. Then
$(\Gg')^{\Pi}=\Gq$, and consequently the exploration set
(Eq.~\ref{eq:es_gate}), every identification formula, every prior
(Eq.~\ref{eq:two_tier_prior}), and --- given the same data and seeds --- the
entire \QCBO{} trajectory are identical under $\Gg$ and $\Gg'$: all of
\QCBO{}'s graph-dependent components are functions of $(\Pi,\Gq)$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:contract} applies. More generally, the conclusion holds for
\emph{any} acyclic $\Gg'$ with $(\Gg')^{\Pi}=\Gq$,
i.e.\ for every quotient-invisible perturbation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[What invariance buys, and its exact scope]
\label{rem:structural_robustness}
Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance} is a statement about the \emph{method}, not about
estimation error: \QCBO{} cannot represent an intra-cluster hypothesis, so no
intra-cluster mistake can affect it. For example, on $\mathbf{X}=\{A,B,C,D\}$
with $\Pi=\{\{A,B\},\{C,D\}\}$, the chains $A\to B\to C\to D$ and
$B\to A\to D\to C$ have the same quotient $\{A,B\}\to\{C,D\}$: \QCBO{} cannot
distinguish them and behaves identically under either. The precise condition is that the perturbed
edge be \emph{invisible in the quotient}: this includes intra-cluster edges and
inter-cluster edges that are \emph{redundant} (a parallel edge from another
cluster member keeps the quotient edge alive). A quotient-\emph{visible} edge is
\emph{not} protected, and \S\ref{sec:exp-scope} shows that the same bow
misspecification, when its endpoints fall in different clusters, removes a
cluster-level arm from the identifiable exploration set.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[How a wrong graph enters the algorithm]
\label{rem:taxonomy}
This taxonomy concerns causal Bayesian optimizers in general, not only \QCBO{}:
a wrong assumed graph reaches any of them through its \emph{derived causal-effect
estimators}, in two ways. \emph{Prior bias:} for any arm whose identifying
estimand references the misspecified edge, the do-calculus estimate used as the GP prior
mean is biased --- even though the intervention is reachable and the \emph{true} effect is
unchanged; expected improvement then wastes evaluations until the interventional data
correct it, a convergence cost paid in GAP/PA-GAP. \emph{Search-set change:} the
exploration set is itself derived from the assumed graph --- by (PO)MIS pruning
in full-DAG \CBO{} \citep{lee2018structural,aglietti2020causal}, and by the
identifiability gate (Eq.~\ref{eq:es_gate}) in \QCBO{} and in the shared backend
of \S\ref{sec:exp} --- so the wrong graph
can flip a verdict and remove an arm (or admit a spurious one). Removing the optimal \emph{singleton} loses
the optimum only when the intervention budget forbids the larger identifiable arm that
reaches the same optimum; absent such a budget, the optimizer must fall back to that arm at a
\emph{sample-efficiency} cost. Coarsening closes both channels whenever the
misspecified edges are quotient-invisible (Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}): the quotient never
references the edge, so no estimator it forms is biased and no identifiability verdict it
makes is flipped.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Validity is not refinement-monotone]
\label{prop:refinement_validity}
There exist a valid partition $\Pi$ and a refinement $\Pi'\preceq\Pi$ whose
quotient $\mathcal{G}^{\Pi'}$ is cyclic; hence refinement does not preserve
validity. Validity is nonetheless decidable from cluster-level structure alone, so
it must be re-verified after each refinement.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity under valid refinement]
\label{prop:refinement}
Let $\Pi'\preceq\Pi$ with both valid, and take $m=\infty$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ccbo_exploration}. Then (i) $\ESpi_0\subseteq\ES^{\Pi'}_{0}$;
(ii) every $\mathbf{X}_s\in\ESpi$ is also in $\ES^{\Pi'}$, i.e.\ effects identifiable
from $\Gq$ remain identifiable from $\mathcal{G}^{\Pi'}$, with the same functional.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Price of coarsening]
\label{prop:price}
Let $\mu^\star(\mathbf{X}_s)=\min_{\mathbf{x}_s\in D(\mathbf{X}_s)}
\E[Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s=\mathbf{x}_s)]$ and
$V(\Pi)=\min_{\mathbf{X}_s\in\ESpi_0}\mu^\star(\mathbf{X}_s)$, with $m=\infty$. For any valid
$\Pi'\preceq\Pi$, $V(\Pi_\bot)\le V(\Pi')\le V(\Pi)$, and $V(\Pi)=V(\Pi_\bot)$ iff
some optimizer $\mathbf{X}_s^\star$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cbo_obj} is a union of $\Pi$-clusters.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Refining the partition: hierarchical \QCBO{}}
\label{sec:refine}
Prop.~\ref{prop:price} prices the commitment: when no optimizer is a union of
$\Pi$-clusters, a fixed-partition run pays $V(\Pi)-y^\star>0$ \emph{forever} ---
its incumbent-based cumulative regret grows linearly,
$R_T/T\to V(\Pi)-y^\star$. Full-DAG \CBO{} has no such floor: its arm space
always contains an optimal arm, so a wrong fine graph biases priors and
misdirects search (Remark~\ref{rem:taxonomy}) but is eventually overridden by
interventional data. Coarse \QCBO{}'s lossy-partition failure is different in
kind --- a \emph{missing arm}, which no amount of data can restore. Refinement
converts the uncorrectable kind of misspecification (arm deletion) back into
the correctable kind, by treating $\Pi$ as a starting resolution rather than a
terminal commitment.

\textbf{Input.} Besides \QCBO{}'s inputs, the practitioner may declare a
\emph{hierarchy} $H$: for each cluster $C\in\Pi$, an optional sub-partition
$H(C)$ of $C$ (default: singletons). No new graph is supplied --- the refined
C-DAG is the quotient of the same assumed structure under the refined
partition, exactly as in Algorithm~\ref{alg:ccbo}.

\textbf{Trigger and step.} \QCBO{}-refine runs Algorithm~\ref{alg:ccbo} at
$\Pi$ and monitors the incumbent every trial: if it improves by less than
$\delta$ over the last $k$ trials ($k{=}5$, $\delta{=}10^{-3}$, tuned once on
one seed and then fixed across all datasets and seeds), each cluster of the
incumbent arm with a declared sub-partition is replaced by it:
$\Pi'=(\Pi\setminus\{C\})\cup H(C)$. Until the trigger, the run is
byte-identical to fixed-coarse \QCBO{} by construction. Validity is re-checked on the new
quotient --- refinement does not preserve acyclicity
(Prop.~\ref{prop:refinement_validity}) --- and an invalid split is
\emph{refused}, the run continuing at $\Pi$. On acceptance the exploration set
is rebuilt at $\Pi'$ and \emph{unioned} with every previously explored arm
(under a finite cluster budget $m$ the superset of
Prop.~\ref{prop:refinement}(i) need not hold verbatim; the union restores it
operationally); all interventional data carry over keyed by arm --- shared
arms keep identical identification functionals
(Prop.~\ref{prop:refinement}(ii)) --- and only the arms new at $\Pi'$ receive
the same $n_0$ initialization points every arm received at $t=0$ (on
\textsc{ClusterBench10L}: $12$ new arms, $60$ extra evaluations, disclosed
wherever compared). Optimization continues warm-started; we evaluate a single
refinement step.

\begin{remark}[Phase-scoped invariance]
\label{rem:phase_invariance}
Let a \QCBO{}-refine run visit partitions $\Pi_0\succeq\Pi_1$. Any
misspecification of the fine graph that is quotient-invisible at \emph{every}
visited partition --- in particular, any edit inside a cluster that is never
split --- leaves the entire trajectory unchanged, including the trigger time
and the refinement decision: per phase this is Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance},
and the trigger and split are functions of the trajectory and the declared
$H$ alone, so the phases agree by induction. Refining a cluster \emph{spends}
its invariance: edits inside a split cluster are invisible before the trigger
and exposed after --- resolution is bought with exposure, cluster by cluster.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The price becomes a transient]
\label{rem:regret}
If the identifiable exploration set at some visited partition contains an
optimal arm (with singleton leaves this holds by Prop.~\ref{prop:identity}
whenever it holds for full-DAG \CBO{}), then $V$ at that partition equals
$y^\star$ (Prop.~\ref{prop:price}) and, whenever the base optimizer is
consistent on a finite arm set, the incumbent converges to $y^\star$:
cumulative regret stops growing, $R_T \le t_r\,r_0 + o(T)$ with $t_r$ the
trigger time and $r_0$ the initial incumbent's regret --- whereas the
fixed-partition run pays $\Theta(T)$. The trigger cannot distinguish
``converged at $V(\Pi)$'' from ``converged at $y^\star$'' (it never observes
$y^\star$), so it also fires on lossless partitions; there refinement costs
only the new arms' initialization and, empirically, a small polish tax
(\S\ref{sec:exp-partition}).
\end{remark}

\section{The second instantiation: model-based \QCBO{}}
\label{sec:qmcbo}
Model-based \CBO{} (MCBO; \citealp{sussex2023model}) couples the surrogate to
the graph more tightly than any arm-based method: it fits one GP per
\emph{mechanism} --- node $k$'s GP maps (parent values, actions) to $k$'s
value --- and evaluates interventions by composing these mechanism GPs along
the DAG. The graph is therefore the surrogate's \emph{architecture}: a wrong
intra-cluster edge corrupts the factorization every prediction flows through,
a failure mode deeper than the prior-bias / arm-deletion taxonomy of
Remark~\ref{rem:taxonomy} and the reason we excluded MCBO from the
fixed-objective protocol of \S\ref{sec:exp}. It is exactly the method class
for which the input contract of Theorem~\ref{thm:contract} matters most.

\textbf{Construction (\QMCBO{}).} Coarsen the factorization, not the
algorithm: one \emph{joint} mechanism per cluster,
$P(\mathbf{X}_C \mid \mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{pa}(C)})$, modeled as a multi-output
GP over $C$'s member coordinates whose inputs are the members of $C$'s parent
clusters (ICM task kernel across coordinates; singleton clusters reduce to
MCBO's per-node GPs), composed along the C-DAG with joint within-cluster
sampling; do() targets are lifted to whole-cluster patterns. Operationally
this is a transformation of MCBO's environment interface --- each node's
mechanism conditions on the union of its parent clusters' members --- so the
stock optimization loop runs unchanged; no component reads an intra-cluster
edge.

\begin{corollary}[\QMCBO{} inherits the contract]
\label{cor:qmcbo_invariance}
Every graph-dependent component of \QMCBO{} (mechanism factorization, target
set, propagation order) is a function of $(\Pi,\Gq)$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:contract} applies: quotient-invisible edits leave its entire
trajectory unchanged. Moreover, (i) at $\Pi=\Pi_\bot$, \QMCBO{} is exactly
MCBO (the quotient parents equal the fine parents and the lifted targets
coincide); and (ii) \QMCBO{}'s arm space equals \QCBO{}-coarse's, so the
price of coarsening of Prop.~\ref{prop:price} applies verbatim: on a lossy
partition, no amount of data recovers $y^\star$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Soundness of cluster mechanisms]
\label{prop:qsound}
Assume causal sufficiency and a valid $\Pi$. For every union-of-clusters
intervention $\doo(\mathbf{X}_S{=}\mathbf{x}_S)$, the composition of the true
joint cluster mechanisms along the C-DAG with the intervened clusters clamped
--- the cluster-level truncated factorization --- equals the interventional
distribution $P(\mathbf{V}\mid\doo(\mathbf{x}_S))$. (Proof:
App.~\ref{app:proofs}.) The per-coordinate variant (independent outputs given
cluster inputs) is its approximation, exact when clusters contain no
intra-cluster edges; we ablate the two in \S\ref{sec:exp}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Exposure: sub-cluster interventions are not identifiable
from cluster mechanisms]
\label{prop:qexposure}
There exist SCMs $M_1, M_2$, causally sufficient and valid for the same
$\Pi$, with identical joint cluster mechanisms and identical interventional
distributions for every union-of-clusters intervention, yet
$P^{M_1}(Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{x}_A)) \neq P^{M_2}(Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{x}_A))$ for
a strict subset $A\subsetneq C$ of some cluster. (Proof: App.~\ref{app:proofs},
by a two-variable linear-Gaussian cluster whose internal edge direction is
unidentified from the joint.) Consequently \emph{no} quotient-respecting
method --- arm-based or model-based --- can evaluate sub-cluster arms from
its inputs alone: resolution below the committed partition must be bought
with intra-cluster structure (the hierarchy $H$ of \S\ref{sec:refine}, whose
phase-scoped invariance prices that purchase) or with interventional data on
the finer arms. The exposure trade of \S\ref{sec:refine} is thus a property
of the quotient contract itself, not of our refinement procedure.
\end{proposition}

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}
Our evaluation is organized around the damage taxonomy (Remark~\ref{rem:taxonomy}).
We first establish the head-to-head methodology that makes a comparison under
misspecification meaningful (\S\ref{sec:exp-setup}); we then show that a
within-cluster error perturbs the convergence (GAP, PA-GAP) of the full-DAG
method while leaving \QCBO{}-coarse unchanged (\S\ref{sec:exp-headline}),
characterize \emph{exactly} which misspecifications are protected and exhibit the
boundary where \QCBO{}-coarse itself breaks (\S\ref{sec:exp-scope}), and quantify
the sample-efficiency benefit (\S\ref{sec:exp-eff}). We close by deliberately
breaking the method's own assumption --- a lossy and a wrong partition
(\S\ref{sec:exp-partition}).

\paragraph{Fixed-objective protocol.}
A head-to-head under misspecification is only meaningful if every
method optimizes the \emph{same} objective; only the \emph{structure each
method reasons with} may be perturbed. We hold fixed the true SCM, the
observational data, the reference optimum $y^\star$, the seeds, and the budget, and
inject the misspecified structure only into the assumed graph each causal method
reasons with. We verify that this
injection leaves the objective \emph{byte-identical} under every perturbation
(App.~\ref{app:seam}); otherwise a performance change could not be
attributed to the misspecified structure rather than to a changed problem.

\paragraph{Perturbations, methods, and metrics.}
We perturb the DAG by adding, deleting, or reversing edges, classified by whether
their endpoints lie within a coarse cluster (\emph{intra}) or across clusters
(\emph{inter}); the headline perturbation is a \emph{bow} --- a directed edge
added on a latently-confounded pair, which renders the corresponding
$\doo$-effect non-identifiable. We compare four methods sharing one estimator
backend, seeds, and initialization, differing only in the causal prior and the graph
each identifies on (Table~\ref{tab:methods}). \BO{} reads no structure;
\CBO{}~\citep{aglietti2020causal} runs the complete identification algorithm on the
full assumed DAG, which by Prop.~\ref{prop:identity} is exactly \QCBO{} at the
finest partition $\Pi_\bot$, so \CBO{} and \QCBO{}-finest are the same algorithm
and coincide
on \emph{every} graph (we list both to make the equivalence explicit);
\QCBO{}-coarse runs that same algorithm on the quotient.

We score trajectories with the survey's GAP/PA-GAP protocol. Let $y_0$ be the
initial incumbent,
$y^{\mathrm{best}}_t$ the best-so-far value after trial $t$, and
$\rho_t=\min\{(y_0-y^{\mathrm{best}}_t)/(y_0-y^\star),\,1\}$ the clipped
improvement ratio (minimization shown; the sign flips for maximization). With
$t^\star$ the first trial attaining the final ratio $\rho_T$ ($t^\star{=}T$ if
there is no improvement),
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{GAP}&=\frac{\rho_T+(T-t^\star)/T}{1+(T-1)/T}\in[0,1],\\
\text{PA-GAP}&=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\rho_t\,\frac{T-t+1}{T}
\in\Big[0,\tfrac{T+1}{2T}\Big].
\end{align*}
GAP combines final improvement with how early it is attained; PA-GAP weights
every trial's improvement by a linearly decaying factor, so it is sensitive to
the whole path. Higher is better for both; GAP@$k$ is GAP computed on the first
$k$ trials.
We report the paired-by-seed degradation
$\Delta=\mathrm{metric}(\text{misspecified},s)-\mathrm{metric}(\text{correct},s)$
for final $Y$, GAP, and PA-GAP, so that per-seed noise cancels; for GAP-type
metrics a \emph{negative} $\Delta$ is a degradation. Alongside we report
standard regret: the simple regret
$r_T=|y^{\mathrm{best}}_T-y^\star|$ of the final incumbent and the cumulative
regret $R_T=\sum_{t=1}^{T} r_t$ of the incumbent sequence.

\subsection{Coarsening guarantees an unchanged trajectory}
\label{sec:exp-headline}
We first fix the \emph{correct} DAG and read off sample efficiency
(Table~\ref{tab:headline}): the causal methods exploit the observational prior to
converge within a handful of interventions, while non-causal \BO{}, which must learn
the ten-dimensional response from scratch, is far slower and worst in absolute $Y$.

We then add the intra-cluster bow (add $X_1\!\to\!X_2$ on the $U_{12}$-confounded
pair) with the objective held fixed. No method's \emph{final} value is
damaged: the optimum $\doo(C_1)$ intervenes on the whole confounded cluster, which
severs the bow for every method. The damage instead appears in \emph{convergence},
where Remark~\ref{rem:taxonomy} predicts it. \QCBO{}-coarse, for which the bow lies inside
$C_1$ and is invisible to the quotient, is \emph{byte-for-byte identical} to the
correctly-specified run --- its trajectory, GAP, and PA-GAP are unchanged
($\Delta\mathrm{GAP}=\Delta\text{PA-GAP}=0$,
Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}). Full-DAG
\CBO{}/\QCBO{}-finest \emph{deletes} every arm the bow touches ---
$\doo(X_1)$ and every superset that leaves $X_2$ unintervened become
unidentifiable and leave the exploration set --- so its GAP and PA-GAP
\emph{shift} (Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}; App.~\ref{app:fulltables}). On this SCM the
shift even helps: $\doo(X_1)$ is a decoy --- the strongest singleton, yet bounded
away from the optimum --- so deleting it concentrates the budget earlier on the
larger arms that contain the optimum, visible as the faster misspecified regret
decay in Fig.~\ref{fig:overlays}.

\paragraph{The same edit with the opposite sign.}
That the deletion \emph{helped} is a property of this SCM, not of the mechanism.
\textsc{ClusterBench10D} (App.~\ref{app:scm}) keeps the structure, mechanisms,
and confounding but moves $Y$'s centers so that the strongest singleton \emph{is}
the global optimum: $\doo(X_1{=}2)$ attains $y^\star{=}0$ (every other singleton
is $\ge 14$), and $\doo(C_1)$ attains the same value, so the coarse partition
remains lossless. Now the identical intra bow deletes the cheap one-dimensional
optimal arm, and full-DAG \CBO{} must reach the optimum through the
higher-dimensional $\doo(X_1,X_2)$ and $\doo(C_1)$: its cumulative regret rises
from $2.4\pm0.6$ to $11.1\pm2.1$ (Table~\ref{tab:damage}) --- a slowdown paid on
every trial, which interventional data never repairs because the arm itself is
gone; GAP, which saturates once the optimum is eventually found, barely registers
it. \QCBO{}-coarse is byte-identical on both variants. The two SCMs are the two
signs of one mechanism --- deleting a decoy accelerates, deleting the optimal arm
injures --- and which case one is in cannot be known without the very structure in
doubt; Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance} removes the question for every
quotient-invisible error.

\subsection{Which misspecifications are protected?}
\label{sec:exp-scope}
A single controlled contrast delimits the scope: the
\emph{same} operation --- adding a directed edge on a latently-confounded pair ---
is invisible to the quotient when intra-cluster and visible when it spans clusters.
Adding $X_1\!\to\!X_2$ (intra $C_1$, on the $U_{12}$-confounded pair) leaves
\QCBO{}-coarse unchanged ($\Delta\mathrm{GAP}=\Delta\text{PA-GAP}=0$). Adding
$X_1\!\to\!X_5$ (across $C_1,C_2$, on the $U_{15}$-confounded pair) forms a
\emph{cluster-level} bow $C_1\to C_2$ with $C_1\leftrightarrow C_2$, which makes
$\doo(C_1)$ --- the arm \QCBO{}-coarse relies on --- non-identifiable; the guarantee
is lost and \QCBO{}-coarse's trajectory moves off zero. Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}
contrasts full-DAG \CBO{} (\QCBO{}-finest), whose GAP/PA-GAP shift under \emph{every}
edge it can see, against \QCBO{}-coarse, whose $\Delta$ is exactly zero on every
quotient-invisible perturbation (intra $\to$ immune; inter-but-redundant $\to$ immune)
and moves off zero only under the inter bow. The other quotient-visible
perturbation, the inter edge-deletion, is not covered by the guarantee, yet its
trajectory is unchanged on this SCM: the deleted
edge never enters the identification of an arm the optimizer uses.
Quotient-invisibility
is thus a \emph{sufficient} condition for invariance, not a necessary one.
The field also contains the most realistic error of all: omitting the latent
confounder $X_1\!\leftrightarrow\!X_2$ from the assumed graph ($P_7$). No
identifiability verdict flips --- the damage channel is pure prior bias --- yet
the full-DAG method's whole trajectory reshuffles ($\Delta\mathrm{GAP}
{=}{+}0.098{\pm}0.038$, with per-seed $\Delta$PA-GAP ranging from $-0.05$ to
$+0.16$), in a direction that varies seed by seed; the quotient discards
intra-cluster bidirected edges, so \QCBO{}-coarse is again byte-identical.
Figure~\ref{fig:dial} shows
the severity dial: \QCBO{}-coarse stays flat at zero across a one-, two-, three-edit
intra-cluster sweep while the full-DAG method's $\Delta$ wanders, and leaves zero
only under the inter-cluster bow.

\subsection{Coarsening also accelerates search}
\label{sec:exp-eff}
The robustness above is achieved at no loss of optimum on ClusterBench10 by design
(the coarse partition is lossless). More generally, coarsening trades resolution
for sample efficiency: with fewer cluster-level arms, a fixed budget is concentrated
rather than spread across a combinatorial candidate family. On six
hard-intervention SCM datasets aggregated from the CBO literature by the survey of
\citet{anonymous2026cbobench}, scored with that survey's common GAP/PA-GAP
protocol against oracle optima,
\QCBO{}-coarse attains a \emph{higher} GAP than \QCBO{}-finest ($\equiv$\CBO{},
Prop.~\ref{prop:identity}) on the higher-dimensional datasets while reaching
comparable final $Y$ (Table~\ref{tab:efficiency}). GAP
rewards early plateaus, so we read the comparison only where final $Y$ is
comparable. Synthetic-2 is the exception: there the domain partition is
\emph{lossy} --- the optimum requires
splitting a cluster --- and \QCBO{}-coarse plateaus at final $Y=-0.511$ versus
$-1.856$ for full-knowledge \CBO{}, the price of coarsening of
Prop.~\ref{prop:price} realized empirically (its GAP is higher only because GAP
rewards the early plateau). \S\ref{sec:exp-partition} quantifies this trade on a
controlled variant of ClusterBench10.

\subsection{When the partition is lossy or wrong}
\label{sec:exp-partition}
Two experiments deliberately violate the assumption the method rests on: that
the partition is correct and the optimum respects it.

\textbf{The price of coarsening, realized.}
\textsc{ClusterBench10L} shares \textsc{ClusterBench10}'s structure but strengthens
the $X_3\!\to\!X_2$ mechanism and restricts hard interventions to a policy box
$[-2,2]$ per variable, engineered so the global optimum $\mathrm{do}(X_1,X_3)$
\emph{splits} the coarse cluster $C_1=\{X_1,X_2,X_3\}$ (oracle-verified: the optimum
is not a union of clusters, is interior to the box, and the coarse-arm price
$V(\Pi)-y^\star=0.95>0$). This is exactly the regime of Prop.~\ref{prop:price}: a
whole-cluster arm $\mathrm{do}(C_1)$ must pin $X_2$ inside the box and cannot reach
the split optimum, whereas full-knowledge \CBO{}$\,\equiv\,$\QCBO{}-finest can. The
realized price appears in Table~\ref{tab:price}: \CBO{} reaches $y^\star$ while
\QCBO{}-coarse plateaus a bounded gap above it (realized price $1.03$, matching the
oracle $0.95$). The plateau is the resolution the
practitioner forfeits by committing to the coarse partition --- paid only where
coarsening is lossy, and exactly $0$ everywhere else (\S\ref{sec:exp-eff}).

\textbf{Refining away the plateau.}
\QCBO{}-refine (\S\ref{sec:refine}) runs the identical coarse configuration
with the singleton hierarchy declared for $C_1$. The plateau trigger fires at
trial $11.1\pm0.8$ (10 seeds), the split passes the validity re-check, and the
warm-started refined phase --- whose exploration set now contains the split
optimum $\mathrm{do}(X_1,X_3)$ --- descends immediately: final
$Y=0.101\pm0.001$, indistinguishable from full-knowledge \CBO{}
($0.103\pm0.001$), against the coarse plateau $1.128\pm0.016$. Cumulative
regret is where the asymptotics live (Remark~\ref{rem:regret}): fixed-coarse
accrues $R_{100}=120.3\pm3.1$ and keeps paying the price ${\approx}1.03$ per
trial forever, while \QCBO{}-refine's $32.1\pm2.6$ has stopped growing --- the
price is paid only until the trigger. \CBO{}'s $5.3\pm0.7$ remains the best
constant: refinement does not out-run a correctly specified full-DAG
optimizer; it removes fixed-coarse's linear regret while phase 1 keeps
coarse's invariance and is byte-identical to it (Remark~\ref{rem:phase_invariance}).
Figure~\ref{fig:refine} overlays the trajectories.
The lossless control and the survey suite complete the picture. On
\textsc{ClusterBench10} (lossless: the optimum is a union of clusters) the
trigger still fires (trial $10.8\pm0.6$) --- it cannot observe $y^\star$ ---
and costs nothing: final $Y=0.070\pm0.021$ vs coarse $0.128\pm0.021$, with
$R_{100}=41.6\pm5.8$ vs $41.8\pm5.2$ (both ahead of \CBO{}'s $71.3\pm5.6$).
Nor is the lossy case an artifact of our construction: on the survey's own
\textsc{synthetic\_2}, whose $\{X,Z\}$ partition is lossy in the wild,
\QCBO{}-refine escapes the coarse plateau ($-0.511$) to the finest-level
optimum ($-1.855$ vs \QCBO{}-finest's $-1.856$; $R_{100}$: $231\pm1$ vs coarse
$346$). On the remaining lossless survey partitions the false-trigger tax is
within noise on \textsc{healthcare} ($5.354\pm0.031$ vs coarse
$5.329\pm0.015$) and small on \textsc{ecology} ($4.885\pm0.028$ vs
$4.983\pm0.051$, task max) --- the singleton-starvation effect that the
rollback rule of \S\ref{sec:limitations} would bound by construction.
App.~\ref{app:refine} (Table~\ref{tab:refine-suite},
Fig.~\ref{fig:refine-suite}) gives the full cross-dataset trajectories and
regret; the honest summary is that refinement rescues the lossy partitions and
does not harm the lossless ones materially, but neither quotient method
out-performs full-knowledge \CBO{} on the small survey partitions, where too
few clusters leave little for coarsening's focus to buy.

\subsection{The contract in the model-based family}
\label{sec:exp-qmcbo}
We run \QMCBO{} against MCBO through MCBO's own harness and protocol
(hallucinated GP network, $\beta{=}10$, $T{=}100$, $5$ seeds) on three of its
environments that re-implement survey datasets: \textsc{ToyGraph} and
\textsc{Synthetic\_2} (partition $\{X,Z\}$) and \textsc{PSAGraph}/healthcare
($\{$Aspirin, Statin$\}$); causal sufficiency holds in all three, matching
Prop.~\ref{prop:qsound}'s assumption. \QMCBO{} differs from MCBO \emph{only}
in the model's graph view and the lifted targets --- a pure architecture
ablation.

\textbf{Invariance at machine precision (Table~\ref{tab:qmcbo-e2}).} Under an
intra-cluster misspecification of the model's view (delete $X{\to}Z$ on
\textsc{ToyGraph}; reverse it on \textsc{Synthetic\_2}; add a spurious
Aspirin${\to}$Statin on \textsc{PSAGraph}), \QMCBO{}'s trajectories are
byte-identical to their correct-graph runs on $15/15$ (env, seed) pairs ---
Cor.~\ref{cor:qmcbo_invariance} realized --- while MCBO's shift on $14/15$
(the one coincidence is chance: its node GPs did change parents). The
direction of MCBO's shift is not predictable --- on these SCMs the wrong
graph sometimes \emph{helped} --- which is precisely why the guarantee is
stated about behavior, not about harm.

\textbf{Soundness matters (Table~\ref{tab:qmcbo-e1}).} On \textsc{ToyGraph},
whose $\{X,Z\}$ cluster contains the only intra-cluster edge, the joint
cluster mechanism reaches the optimum exactly ($2.172{\pm}0.000$, every
seed) where MCBO attains $1.42$ and the per-coordinate ablation $1.26$ ---
the within-cluster residual correlation of Prop.~\ref{prop:qsound} is the
missing ingredient, and it is aleatoric at fixed cluster inputs, so it must
be carried by a full-rank task-noise model rather than the latent kernel. On
\textsc{Synthetic\_2} the partition is lossy (the optimum is the sub-cluster
arm $\doo(Z)$), and all quotient variants sit at the same $V(\Pi)$ plateau
irrespective of mechanism quality --- Prop.~\ref{prop:price} through the
model-based lens, and Prop.~\ref{prop:qexposure}'s floor in action. On
\textsc{PSAGraph}, \QMCBO{} edges out MCBO ($-4.26$ vs $-4.34$,
$y^\star{=}{-}4.13$) at a third of the wall-clock: coarsening the
factorization also concentrates the model.

The survey of \citet{anonymous2026cbobench} organizes the \CBO{} landscape along
design axes --- graph knowledge, intervention representation, surrogate, decision
rule --- and its ``unknown causal structure'' family contains only methods that
\emph{learn} or \emph{average} graphs (\CEO{}, GACBO, Durand et al.); it names
robustness to structural error and scalable unknown-graph optimization among the
field's open problems. \QCBO{} occupies an unfilled cell on the graph-knowledge
axis: it neither assumes the full DAG nor learns one, but takes a coarser, more
reliable input (a partition + C-DAG) and identifies on the quotient. Methods that
relax \CBO{}'s full-graph requirement either learn structure online --- GACBO
maintains a posterior over DAGs at substantial per-trial cost
\citep{mukherjee2024gacbo}, \CEO{} keeps a graph posterior and hedges through an
entropy-based acquisition \citep{branchini2023ceo}, and \citet{durand2025unknown}
identify $\Pa(Y)$ under a $Y$-leaf assumption --- or assume a known equivalence class
\citep{park2025dmis}; even contextual \CBO{} \citep{arsenyan2026contextual} assumes
the full graph. Graph \emph{averaging}
\citep[\CEO{};][]{branchini2023ceo} offers a different kind of protection:
it hedges over a candidate set of full DAGs at per-trial cost and only
dilutes a wrong edge in proportion to the posterior mass on graphs lacking it,
whereas \QCBO{} \emph{commits} to a coarser-but-correct quotient, pays nothing
per trial, and is exactly invariant to any edit the quotient does not resolve
(Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance})---a qualitatively different guarantee from
posterior-weighted dilution. The identification machinery we
rely on is established --- C-DAG do-calculus \citep{anand2023cdag}, its extension
to C-DMGs over ADMGs \citep{ferreira2025cdmg}, cluster-level Markov properties
\citep{wahl2024foundations}, relaxed partition admissibility
\citep{yvernes2025relaxing}, and C-DAGs as discovery background knowledge
\citep{ruizdevargas2025cdags} --- but none of these perform optimization. Closest in
spirit is the concurrent abstraction-based proposal of \citet{zeitler2025mfacbo},
which coarsens the \emph{outcome} as a cheap proxy
\citep{poloczek2017multi,beckers2019approximate}; \QCBO{} is complementary in that
it coarsens the manipulable \emph{inputs}.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
Theorem~\ref{thm:contract} is structural, not statistical: coarsening acts as
a hard regularizer on the hypothesis space, trading resolution
(Prop.~\ref{prop:price}) for immunity to a class of misspecifications.
Caveats, briefly. (i) The invariance belongs to the \emph{quotient}, not our
implementations: the finest instantiations ($\equiv$\CBO{}/MCBO,
Prop.~\ref{prop:identity}, Cor.~\ref{cor:qmcbo_invariance}) have no such
guarantee, so robustness is read off the finest-to-coarse contrast, not
absolute numbers. (ii) The guarantee is about \emph{behavior}: a
within-cluster error shifts the full-DAG method's convergence but not
necessarily its converged value under our identical-action-space protocol;
invariance holds in either regime. (iii) \BO{} is trivially robust but pays
heavily in absolute performance; robustness is not ignoring structure.
(iv) DCBO is excluded from the fixed-objective protocol (per-environment
coupling); MCBO now appears through its own harness in
\S\ref{sec:exp-qmcbo}. (v) Faithfulness does not transfer to the cluster
level \citep{wahl2024foundations}: cluster-level independences must not be
read back as fine structure. (vi) Unit intervention costs only;
cost-asymmetric settings move where coarsening pays. (vii) The
identifiability gate costs $\sum_{k\le m}\binom{K}{k}$ complete-ID checks on
the \emph{quotient} --- polynomial in clusters for fixed $m$, precomputed,
set by the coarse partition rather than the fine dimension.
(viii) \emph{\QMCBO{} assumes causal sufficiency} (Prop.~\ref{prop:qsound}),
as MCBO itself does; bidirected C-DAG edges require correlated-noise cluster
mechanisms and are the model-based family's open problem, where the
arm-based family's identification gate already handles them.
(ix) \emph{Asymptotics.} Full-DAG \CBO{} eventually self-corrects under any
wrong graph --- its arm space always contains an optimal arm, and interventional
data override a biased prior --- so a fixed coarse partition's advantages are
finite-budget focus and invariance, never limiting value; on a lossy partition
its cumulative regret is linear in $T$, in both families
(Cor.~\ref{cor:qmcbo_invariance}). Refinement (\S\ref{sec:refine}) removes
the linear term at the cost priced by Prop.~\ref{prop:qexposure} and
Remark~\ref{rem:phase_invariance}: resolution is bought with exposure. The
trigger never observes $y^\star$, so it also fires on lossless partitions,
where the realized cost is the new arms' initialization plus, on one of three
lossless controls (\textsc{ecology}), a small polish tax
(\S\ref{sec:exp-partition}); a rollback rule bounding that tax by
construction, and discovering the hierarchy from the run's own interventional
data, are the natural follow-ups.

% =============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\QCBO{} runs causal Bayesian optimization on a coarser, more reliable structural
commitment --- clusters --- with complete
quotient-level identification, a provable invariance to intra-cluster misspecification, and a
sample-efficiency benefit. Robustness to a wrong graph is not a single property but
a question of \emph{how} the graph enters the algorithm; coarsening removes an
entire class of errors from the input, and the practitioner dials resolution against
robustness by choosing how much structure to commit to --- and, with a declared
hierarchy, can re-dial it mid-run: plateau-triggered refinement turns the price of
a too-coarse commitment into a bounded transient instead of a permanent regret rate.

\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}
Notation is as in \S\ref{sec:setup} and \S\ref{sec:theory}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:contract}]
As in the proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance} below, $(\Gg')^{\Pi}=\Gq$
whenever $\Gg'$ differs from $\Gg$ by edits confined within clusters or
redundant across them: the latent projection never uses an
observable--observable edge as an interior path vertex, and the quotient
keeps a cluster-level edge iff \emph{some} fine edge crosses the cluster
pair. It remains to show trajectory equality. Write $\mathcal{A}$'s run as a
sequence of decisions $d_1, d_2, \dots$ (which arm/action to evaluate,
model refits, stopping); by hypothesis each $d_t$ is a deterministic function
$d_t = f_t\big((\Pi,\Gq),\, \mathcal{D}_{t-1},\, \omega_t\big)$ of the
quotient, the data collected so far, and the RNG stream. Under $\Gg$ and
$\Gg'$ the first arguments coincide; the objective (the true SCM) and the
seeds coincide by the fixed-objective protocol, so
$\mathcal{D}_0, \omega_1$ coincide, hence $d_1$ coincides, hence
$\mathcal{D}_1$ coincides; induction over $t$ gives equality of the entire
trajectory.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:qsound}]
Under causal sufficiency the fine SCM is Markov relative to $\Gg$ with
mutually independent exogenous noises, so for any intervention
$\doo(\mathbf{x}_S)$ on a union of clusters $S$ the fine truncated
factorization gives
$P(\mathbf{V}\mid\doo(\mathbf{x}_S))
 = \prod_{k\notin S} P\big(X_k \mid \mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{pa}_{\Gg}(k)}\big)
 \big|_{\mathbf{X}_S=\mathbf{x}_S}$.
Group the factors by the clusters of $\Pi$. For a cluster $C\not\subseteq S$,
the product of its members' factors is, by the chain rule applied in any
topological order of $\Gg$ restricted to $C$, exactly the joint conditional
$P\big(\mathbf{X}_C \mid \mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{pa}(C)}\big)$, where
$\mathrm{pa}(C)$ --- the union of $C$'s parent clusters --- contains every
out-of-cluster variable appearing in a member's conditioning set (validity of
$\Pi$ guarantees the quotient is acyclic, so $\mathrm{pa}(C)$ is well defined
and precedes $C$ in C-DAG order). The fine factorization therefore equals the
product over clusters of the joint cluster mechanisms with the clusters of
$S$ clamped at $\mathbf{x}_S$ --- the cluster-level truncated factorization.
Note both uses of sufficiency: independent noises across clusters (no
bidirected edges) and the absence of unobserved common causes that would make
a member's conditional depend on variables outside
$\mathrm{pa}(C)\cup C$. The per-coordinate variant replaces
$P(\mathbf{X}_C\mid\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{pa}(C)})$ by the product of its
marginals, exact iff the members are conditionally independent given
$\mathrm{pa}(C)$ --- in particular when $C$ contains no intra-cluster edges.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:qexposure}]
Take $\mathbf{X}=\{X_2,X_3\}$ as one root cluster $C$, target
$Y = X_2 + \varepsilon_Y$ its child, all noises standard normal and
independent (sufficiency holds; any $\Pi$ clustering $\{X_2,X_3\}$ is valid).
Let $M_1$: $X_2=\varepsilon_2$, $X_3 = a X_2 + \varepsilon_3$; and $M_2$:
$X_3=\varepsilon_3'$, $X_2 = a' X_3 + \varepsilon_2'$, with
$a' = a/(1+a^2)$, $\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_3') = 1+a^2$, and
$\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_2') = 1 - a^2/(1+a^2)$. Both induce the same
zero-mean Gaussian joint on $(X_2,X_3)$ --- covariance
$\begin{psmallmatrix}1 & a\\ a & 1+a^2\end{psmallmatrix}$ --- hence the same
joint cluster mechanism (here, the same root-cluster distribution), and the
same $P(Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{x}_C))$ for the whole-cluster intervention (both
set $X_2$ directly). But under the sub-cluster intervention $\doo(X_3=x)$:
in $M_1$, $X_2=\varepsilon_2$ is unaffected, so
$\mathbb{E}[Y\mid\doo(X_3{=}x)]=0$ for all $x$; in $M_2$,
$X_2 = a'x$, so $\mathbb{E}[Y\mid\doo(X_3{=}x)] = a'x \neq 0$ for
$x\neq 0$, $a\neq 0$. Every quotient-respecting input --- the C-DAG, the
joint cluster mechanisms, all whole-cluster interventional distributions ---
coincides between $M_1$ and $M_2$, yet the sub-cluster query differs;
no function of those inputs can answer it.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:identity}]
Singleton clusters make the quotient map the identity on $\Gg^{*}$, and unions of
at most $m$ singletons are exactly the subsets of size at most $m$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}]
The latent projection removes only latent vertices; an edge between two observables
is never an interior vertex of a projection path, so $(\Gg')^{*}$ and $\Gg^{*}$
differ exactly by the modified intra-cluster edges. The quotient keeps a directed
(resp.\ bidirected) cluster-level edge iff some fine edge crosses two \emph{distinct}
clusters; edges within a cluster contribute nothing. Hence the quotients coincide.
All remaining objects --- $\ESpi$, ID formulas, priors --- are functions of $\Gq$
and data alone.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:refinement_validity}]
Take $\mathbf{X}=\{a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2\}$ with fine edges $a_1\to b_1$ and $b_2\to a_2$.
The coarsest partition $\Pi=\{\{a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2\}\}$ is valid: both edges are
intra-cluster, so the quotient has a single manipulable node and is acyclic. Its
refinement $\Pi'=\{\{a_1,a_2\},\{b_1,b_2\}\}$ exposes both edges as inter-cluster ---
$a_1\to b_1$ contributes $A\to B$ and $b_2\to a_2$ contributes $B\to A$ --- inducing
the 2-cycle $A\rightleftarrows B$, so $\mathcal{G}^{\Pi'}$ is cyclic and $\Pi'$ is
invalid. Decidability is immediate from the acyclicity clause of
Def.~\ref{def:coarsening}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:refinement}]
(i) A union of $\Pi$-clusters is a union of $\Pi'$-clusters because each
$\Pi$-cluster is. (ii) Quotienting is compositional: $\Gq$ is the quotient of
$\mathcal{G}^{\Pi'}$ under the induced partition of $\Pi'$-clusters, so every fine
graph compatible with $\mathcal{G}^{\Pi'}$ is compatible with $\Gq$. Identifiability
from $\Gq$ means a single functional of the observational distribution equals
$\E[Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s)]$ across all SCMs whose graph is compatible with $\Gq$
\citep{anand2023cdag}; a fortiori the same functional works across the smaller class
compatible with $\mathcal{G}^{\Pi'}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:price}]
By Prop.~\ref{prop:refinement}(i), $\ESpi_0\subseteq\ES^{\Pi'}_0\subseteq
\ES^{\Pi_\bot}_0=2^{\mathbf{X}}\setminus\{\emptyset\}$; minima over larger
sets are no larger. Equality holds iff the unrestricted argmin intersects $\ESpi_0$,
which is exactly the stated condition.
\end{proof}

\section{ClusterBench10: specification and oracle}\label{app:scm}

\paragraph{Variables and partition.}
ClusterBench10 has five manipulable variables $\mathbf{X}=\{X_1,\dots,X_5\}$, four
non-manipulable mediators $\{M_1,M_2,M_3,Z\}$, and the target $Y$, plus two latent
confounders $U_{12}\!\to\!\{X_1,X_2\}$ and $U_{15}\!\to\!\{X_1,X_5\}$ (bidirected
$X_1\!\leftrightarrow\!X_2$ and $X_1\!\leftrightarrow\!X_5$ in the projected ADMG).
The coarse partition is $\Pi=\{C_1,C_2\}\cup\text{singletons}$ with
$C_1=\{X_1,X_2,X_3\}$, $C_2=\{X_4,X_5\}$; all non-manipulable atoms and $Y$ are
singletons. The finest partition $\Pi_\bot$ (all singletons) recovers \CBO{}
(Prop.~\ref{prop:identity}).

\paragraph{Structural equations.}
Roots $X_1,X_3,X_4,X_5$ carry only exogenous noise (and their latents); the remaining
mechanisms are
\[
\begin{array}{llll}
X_2 = 0.3\,X_3, & M_1 = X_3, & M_2 = X_1+X_2, & M_3 = X_4+X_5,\quad Z = X_5,
\end{array}
\]
all linear, and the target is a \emph{quadratic bowl} in its parents,
\[
Y \;=\; \sum_{p\in\Pa(Y)} a_p\,(p-c_p)^2,\qquad
\Pa(Y)=\{X_1,X_2,X_3,M_1,M_2,M_3,Z\},
\]
with curvatures $a=(3.0,1.5,1.0,0.5,0.8,0.3,0.3)$ and centres
$c=(2,1,1,1,3,0,0)$ over $(X_1,X_2,X_3,M_1,M_2,M_3,Z)$. The quadratic form places the
optimum at an \emph{interior} point (not a box vertex), so unstructured \BO{} cannot
solve it in a constant number of evaluations; this is what makes the sample-efficiency
comparison non-degenerate. In the observational sampler every node receives
additive Gaussian noise ($\sigma=0.7$, except $\sigma_Y=1$ and
$\sigma_{X_3}=\sigma_{X_4}=\sigma_{X_5}=1$), and the latents enter their
children with unit loadings, $U_{12}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1.6^2)$ and
$U_{15}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1^2)$ --- the $U_{12}$ scale is what produces
$\mathrm{corr}(X_1,X_2)\approx 0.70$. The latent confounders enter only the
observational
sampler and the declared structure --- the noise-free objective every method optimizes
is the deterministic SEM above, identical across all methods and perturbations
(App.~\ref{app:seam}).

\paragraph{Monte-Carlo oracle (verified at build time).}
The generator's oracle minimizes the noise-free objective over each intervention arm's
box and confirms: (C1) $\doo(X_1)$ is the strongest \emph{singleton} arm (it lowers $Y$
from the no-intervention value $\approx 23.7$ to $\approx 3.6$, a reduction of $20.0$,
versus $8.1$ for $\doo(X_2)$ and $5.6$ for $\doo(X_3)$), so deleting it at the finest
partition is a genuine loss; (C2) the global optimum over all subsets is
$\doo(C_1)=\doo(X_1,X_2,X_3)$ at $(X_1,X_2,X_3)=(2,1,1)$ with $Y^\star=0$ --- a
\emph{union of clusters}, so the coarse partition is lossless (Prop.~\ref{prop:price});
$C_2$ is neutral-to-harmful (its $Y$-terms are minimized at $X_4=X_5=0$), so a method
that intervenes on the full $5$-dimensional box wastes two dimensions; (C3) the optimum
is interior; and the observational sample has $\mathrm{corr}(X_1,X_2)=0.70$ via $U_{12}$,
making the bow a genuine confounded misspecification. All perturbed graphs below are
acyclic. The build script is \texttt{scripts/generate\_clusterbench10.py} and the
structural premises are asserted in \texttt{ccbo/tests/test\_clusterbench10\_structure.py}.

\paragraph{Perturbation taxonomy.}
Each perturbation adds/deletes/reverses one or more edges, classified by whether its
endpoints lie within a coarse cluster (\emph{intra}) or across clusters (\emph{inter}),
and by whether it is \emph{quotient-invisible} (intra, or inter-but-redundant). The
headline contrast is \textbf{P1} (add $X_1\!\to\!X_2$, an intra-$C_1$ bow on the
$X_1\!\leftrightarrow\!X_2$ confounded pair --- makes $\doo(X_1)$ non-identifiable at the
finest partition) versus \textbf{Pic} (add $X_1\!\to\!X_5$, the same operation across
clusters --- forms a cluster-level bow $C_1\!\to\!C_2$ alongside
$C_1\!\leftrightarrow\!C_2$, deleting $\doo(C_1)$ at the coarse level). The remaining
perturbations are P2 (delete $X_3\!\to\!X_2$) and P3 (reverse it), both intra-$C_1$
prior-bias controls; P5 (delete the sole inter edge $X_3\!\to\!M_1$) and P6 (delete the
\emph{redundant} inter edge $X_1\!\to\!M_2$, with $X_2\!\to\!M_2$ surviving so the
quotient is unchanged); P7 (omit the latent confounder $X_1\!\leftrightarrow\!X_2$
from the assumed graph --- the \emph{forgotten confounder}; directed edges and hence
all identifiability verdicts are untouched, so this is a pure prior-bias
perturbation); and a severity sweep S1--S3 of $1,2,3$ simultaneous intra-$C_1$
edits. \QCBO{}-coarse is provably byte-identical on every quotient-invisible
perturbation (P1, P2, P3, P6, P7, S1--S3) and is \emph{not} protected on Pic and P5.

\paragraph{ClusterBench10D (the damage companion).}
Identical structure, mechanisms, noise, and latents; only $Y$'s centers move to
the values $\doo(X_1{=}2)$ induces in the noise-free SEM, $c=(2,0,0,0,2,0,0)$
over $(X_1,X_2,X_3,M_1,M_2,M_3,Z)$. Consequently $\doo(X_1)$ attains the global
optimum $y^\star{=}0$ (the runner-up singleton is $\ge 14$), $\doo(C_1)$ attains
the same value --- the coarse partition stays lossless --- and
$\mathrm{corr}(X_1,X_2)=0.70$ is unchanged. Build-time oracle checks D1--D3
assert exactly these three premises
(\texttt{scripts/generate\_clusterbench10D.py}); the runner asserts
\QCBO{}-coarse's byte-identity under the bow before writing results.

\section{Fixed-objective protocol: per-method details}\label{app:seam}
The invariant is that every method optimizes the \emph{same} objective --- the true
SCM's noise-free \texttt{intervention\_function} --- and only the \emph{structure each
method reasons with} is perturbed. We realize this per method as follows.

\textbf{\QCBO{} (finest and coarse).} The objective is read from the original graph's
SEM, while the structure is supplied separately through the assumed graph name
(\texttt{CoarsenedGraph(\ldots, assumed\_graph\_name=variant)}). Swapping the assumed
graph changes the quotient, the exploration set, the identification formulas, and the
prior, but never the SEM that generates outcomes.

\textbf{\CBO{} (full-DAG identification).} \CBO{} is \QCBO{}'s backend at the
identity partition (Prop.~\ref{prop:identity}): it runs the same complete
identification algorithm, only on the full DAG rather than a quotient. The
injection is the same assumed-graph swap as for \QCBO{} --- the perturbed
structure reaches the exploration set and the identification formulas that build
the prior --- while outcomes are always drawn from the true SEM. Its trajectory
is byte-identical to \QCBO{}-finest on every graph.

\textbf{\BO{} (non-causal control).} \BO{} optimizes a single joint intervention
on all manipulable variables with a zero-mean GP prior
(\texttt{Causal\_prior=False}): it reads no structure at all, so it
is invariant to every perturbation by construction (we run it once and report
$\Delta=0$), at the price of the weakest absolute performance.

\textbf{Byte-identity verification.} For the backend methods the decoupling holds
by construction: outcomes are always evaluated on the true graph's SEM, and the
perturbation enters only through the separately-supplied assumed-graph variant.
As an independent guard, \texttt{seam\_ok} evaluates the objective on every
singleton arm at random points under the true (P0) versus each perturbed
structure and asserts the outcomes match to machine precision. It passes for all
perturbations P1--P6 and S1--S3: feeding a method a wrong DAG changes only its causal
reasoning, never the problem it is solving.

\section{Implementation and reproducibility}\label{app:repro}
All methods share one surrogate/acquisition backend (the benchmark's \CBO{} loop), so
comparisons differ only in the assumed structure. \textbf{Surrogate.} Each
exploration-set arm carries a \textsc{GPy} \texttt{GPRegression} model with an RBF
kernel (signal variance and lengthscale initialized to $1$, ARD off) and observation
noise fixed near zero ($10^{-10}$, matching the deterministic mechanisms); the causal
prior supplies the do-calculus estimate as the GP \emph{mean function}, so
$\Delta$-metrics isolate the effect of a wrong prior/exploration set from surrogate
noise. \textbf{Acquisition.} Causal Expected Improvement, maximized over the
box-constrained intervention domain by gradient-based L-BFGS. \textbf{Protocol.}
Unit per-variable intervention costs; $5$ initial interventional points per arm;
$T{=}100$ trials; $10$ seeds on ClusterBench10/10L/10D and $5$ on the survey suite. GAP and
PA-GAP are defined in \S\ref{sec:exp-setup}; a negative $\Delta$ is a
degradation throughout. \textbf{Invariance tolerance.} Byte-identity of
trajectories holds exactly on all seeds of every protected perturbation except
one seed of the intra bow, where GP-solver floating-point nondeterminism
(independent of the perturbation) perturbs intermediate iterates by
$<\!10^{-8}$ while the recovered optimum is exact; every quotient-visible
perturbation moves trajectories by $\ge\!10^{-2}$. We report invariance at this
tolerance. \textbf{Artifacts.} The ClusterBench10, ClusterBench10L, and ClusterBench10D
generators (with their C1--C3 / L1--L3 / D1--D3 oracle checks), the structure-assertion tests,
the \texttt{seam\_ok} guard, every per-method runner, and the table/figure emitters are
released, so all reported numbers regenerate from the released SCMs and seeds.

\section{The cost of a wrong partition}\label{app:wrongpi}

\emph{partition} is stressed by quotienting the base structure once under

\section{\QCBO{}-refine across datasets}\label{app:refine}
Table~\ref{tab:refine-suite} and Fig.~\ref{fig:refine-suite} give the full
cross-dataset picture for \QCBO{}-refine (\S\ref{sec:refine}): the two
\textsc{ClusterBench} variants and the three survey datasets whose domain
partition we refine ($10$ seeds on the \textsc{ClusterBench} pair, $5$ on the
survey datasets; the finest run supplies the \CBO{} row by
Prop.~\ref{prop:identity}). Three regimes are visible. \emph{(i) Lossless,
partition helps} (\textsc{ClusterBench10}): both quotient methods beat \CBO{}
on final $Y$ and cumulative regret, and refinement adds a little more; the
false trigger --- refine cannot observe $y^\star$, so it fires anyway --- costs
nothing ($R_T\,41.6$ vs coarse $41.8$). \emph{(ii) Lossy}
(\textsc{ClusterBench10L}, \textsc{synthetic\_2}): fixed-coarse plateaus and
its cumulative regret grows linearly; refinement escapes the plateau to
\CBO{}'s optimum ($-1.855$ vs finest $-2.054$ on \textsc{synthetic\_2}) and
bends $R_T$ back toward the better of \{coarse, \CBO{}\}. \emph{(iii) Lossless,
partition neutral} (\textsc{ecology}, \textsc{healthcare}): the four methods
sit within noise on final $Y$; here the false trigger costs a small polish tax
on \textsc{ecology} ($4.885$ vs coarse $4.983$, task max) and nothing
measurable on \textsc{healthcare}. Two honest reads follow. Refinement lowers
GAP relative to fixed-coarse everywhere --- the mid-run split adds arms and
re-explores, trading early-budget GAP for the final-value and regret recovery
--- so it is the right default only when the partition is suspected lossy.
And on the small survey partitions ($2$--$5$ manipulable variables) neither
quotient method dominates full-knowledge \CBO{} on final $Y$; the clear wins
are on the constructed benchmarks, where the cluster count is large enough for
coarsening's focus to register. This is the finite-budget, few-cluster regime;
the $\Theta(K^m)$-vs-$\Theta(N^m)$ arm-count gap that motivates coarsening
widens with problem size, which these $N\!\le\!10$ datasets do not exercise.
