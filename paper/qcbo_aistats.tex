\begin{abstract}
Causal Bayesian optimization (\CBO{}) uses a causal graph to restrict
candidate interventions and to construct priors, but assumes a correct
DAG. We propose coarsening as an input contract: the practitioner
supplies a partition of the manipulable variables and its quotient
graph. If every graph-dependent component factors through this pair,
fine-graph hypotheses with the same quotient produce identical
trajectories. We instantiate the contract for three families of the
field: \QCBO{}, \QDCBO{}, and \QMCBO{}. Each method is invariant to
quotient-invisible edge errors and recovers its base method at the
identity partition. We characterize the action-space cost of restricting
interventions to unions of clusters: under full intervention support and
cluster-contained confounding, coarsening preserves the best attainable
value, while intervention constraints or confounding outside a cluster
can create a positive action-space gap. A fixed partition with a
positive gap incurs linear cumulative regret. Hierarchical refinement
restores vanishing average regret once an optimal arm is exposed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Causal Bayesian optimization searches over interventions $\doo(\mathbf{X}_s=\mathbf{x}_s)$ and uses observational data with a causal graph to construct priors over the interventional response \citep{agliettiCausalBayesianOptimization2020b}. \CBO{} and its descendants \citep{agliettiDynamicCausalBayesian2021a,agliettiConstrainedCausalBayesian2023a,sussexMODELBASEDCAUSALBAYESIAN2023} assume a correct DAG. Stress tests show that method performance can change when only the assumed graph is perturbed \citep{anonymous2026cbobench}. We study \emph{graph misspecification}, where the learner trusts one incorrect graph, and replace fine structure with a coarser structural commitment.

\paragraph{Wrong edges are costly: two-variable example.}
Take manipulable $X_1,X_2$, latently confounded, both parents of
\begin{equation*}
Y=\lambda(X_1-2)^2+(X_2+2)^2+\varepsilon, \qquad y^\star=0.
\end{equation*}
Deleting one edge from the assumed graph removes every $X_1$-containing candidate from the fine search space, and no amount of interventional data restores a candidate the search space does not contain:
\begin{equation*}
X_1\to Y\ \text{deleted} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \min_{x_2}\E[Y\mid\doo(x_2)]=4.25\lambda.
\end{equation*}
Commit instead to the cluster $C_1=\{X_1,X_2\}$: the quotient remains $C_1\to Y$ under both hypotheses (the edge survives through $X_2\to Y$), so a quotient method's input, and hence its trajectory, is unchanged (Fig.~\ref{fig:motivating}). Quotient-visible edits are not protected, and whole-cluster actions can be too coarse. \S\ref{sec:theory} quantifies both boundaries.

\paragraph{Coarser commitments decide less, so less can be wrong.}
A cluster-level structural belief encodes strictly fewer decisions than a
full DAG: one aggregate judgement between two groups of variables stands in
for several edge-level judgements the modeler is never asked to make.
Both ends of this spectrum have been studied: full-DAG \CBO{} at one end, methods
that learn structure online at heavy per-trial cost at the other
\citep{mukherjeeGraphAgnosticCausal2024,branchiniCausalEntropyOptimization2023}. Reliable
cluster-level structure has a developed identification theory
\citep{CausalEffectIdentification,ferreiraIdentifyingMacroCausal2025,wahlFoundationsCausalDiscovery2024} but, to our
knowledge, no optimization method. \QCBO{} fills this gap. The practitioner supplies a partition $\Pi$ of the manipulable variables and the C-DAG $\Gq$ over its clusters (when a fine-grained hypothesis graph is available, $\Gq$ is its quotient, with bidirected edges absorbing latent confounding; \citealp{leeStructuralCausalBandits2019}). \QCBO{} explores cluster-level interventions, the minimal intervention sets of the C-DAG, using identified cluster-level do-calculus estimates as GP prior means where the quotient identifies them, and a common uninformative prior where it does not. This input never looks inside a cluster, so an edge error confined to a cluster cannot even be represented.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Robustness as an input contract.} Quotient invariance is \emph{method-agnostic} (Theorem~\ref{thm:contract}): any optimizer whose graph-dependent components factor through $(\Pi,\Gq)$ is invariant to arbitrary within-cluster edge errors. We instantiate the contract for the arm-based, dynamic, and model-based families (\QCBO{}, \QDCBO{}, \QMCBO{}; \S\ref{sec:method}).
  \item \textbf{Action-space cost.} Coarsening restricts interventions to unions of clusters. We characterize the resulting gap $\Delta(\Pi)$, give sufficient conditions for $\Delta(\Pi)=0$, and show a fixed partition with $\Delta(\Pi)>0$ incurs linear cumulative regret that hierarchical refinement removes (\S\ref{sec:theory}).
  \item \textbf{Experiments.} Three minimal SCMs with closed-form oracles isolate the failure channels one at a time, followed by a portability suite running each quotient method against its base on the base paper's own experiments (\S\ref{sec:exp}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

A structural causal model $\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V},F, P(\mathbf{U})\rangle$ induces a distribution over observables $\mathbf{V}$ and, for any $\mathbf{X}_s\subseteq\mathbf{V}$, interventional distributions $P(\cdot\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s=\mathbf{x}_s))$ obtained by replacing the mechanisms of $\mathbf{X}_s$ with constants \citep{pearlCausality2009}. The induced graph $\Gg$ has a directed edge per functional argument and a bidirected edge per shared latent. Projecting out latents yields an ADMG over $\mathbf{V}$ \citep{leeStructuralCausalBandits2019}. 

\paragraph{Causal Bayesian optimization.}
Given manipulable variables $\mathbf{X}\subseteq\mathbf{V}$ and target $Y$,
\CBO{} \citep{agliettiCausalBayesianOptimization2020b} solves
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:cbo_obj}
  (\mathbf{X}_s^\star,\mathbf{x}_s^\star) =
  \arg\min_{\substack{\mathbf{X}_s\in\ES\\ \mathbf{x}_s\in D(\mathbf{X}_s)}} \E\!\left[Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s=\mathbf{x}_s)\right],
\end{equation}
where $D(\mathbf{X}_s)$ is the interventional domain, maintaining one GP per candidate set $\mathbf{X}_s$ in an exploration set $\ES$ (each a continuous BO sub-problem over $\mathbf{x}_s$), with prior mean the do-calculus estimate from observational data and the assumed graph. The exploration set is pruned structurally: \CBO{} searches over the \emph{minimal intervention sets} (MIS) of the assumed graph \citep{leeStructuralCausalBandits,agliettiCausalBayesianOptimization2020b}. Writing $\Gg_{\bar{\mathbf{X}}_s}$ for the mutilated graph with all edges into $\mathbf{X}_s$ removed and $\An(\cdot)$ for ancestors,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:mis}
  \ES = \mathrm{MIS}(\Gg) = \bigl\{\, \emptyset\neq\mathbf{X}_s\subseteq\mathbf{X} : \mathbf{X}_s\subseteq \An_{\Gg_{\bar{\mathbf{X}}_s}}(Y) \,\bigr\}:
\end{equation}
if some member of $\mathbf{X}_s$ is no ancestor of $Y$ once the whole set is intervened on, $\doo(\mathbf{X}_s)$ induces the same distribution as intervening on a strict subset, so the arm is redundant. The possibly-optimal sets POMIS are a further graph-only pruning with $\mathrm{POMIS}\subseteq\mathrm{MIS}$ \citep{leeStructuralCausalBandits}. Each trial selects the arm and value maximizing cost-normalized causal expected improvement \citep[EI;][]{garnettBayesianOptimization2023}; the model-based family MCBO selects instead by the Gaussian-process upper confidence bound acquisition function \citep[GP-UCB;][]{srinivasGaussianProcessOptimization2010,sussexMODELBASEDCAUSALBAYESIAN2023}. Both the pruning and the priors are functions of the assumed graph, which is where misspecification enters.

\paragraph{Coarsening and C-DAGs.}
\begin{definition}[Coarsening~\citep{madaleno2026a}]
\label{def:coarsening}
A partition $\Pi=\{C_1,\ldots,C_K\}$ of $\mathbf{V}$ induces the map $\chi:\mathbf{V}\to\Pi$ and the quotient graph $\Gq=(\Pi,E^{\Pi})$ of a graph $\Gg=(\mathbf{V},E)$,
\begin{equation*}
E^{\Pi}=\{(\chi(u),\chi(v)) : (u,v)\in E,\ \chi(u)\neq\chi(v)\}.
\end{equation*}
$\Pi$ is \emph{valid} when the directed part of $\Gq$ is acyclic. $\Gq$ is then a \textbf{coarsening} of $\Gg$.
\end{definition}
For ADMGs the quotient additionally carries $C_k\leftrightarrow C_\ell$ iff some $V_i\in C_k,V_j\in C_\ell$ share a bidirected edge. The result is a C-DAG in the sense of \citet{CausalEffectIdentification}: do-calculus on it is sound and complete for the class of fine graphs that quotient to it. Markov properties transfer to the cluster level, faithfulness does not \citep{wahlFoundationsCausalDiscovery2024}.

\paragraph{Four objects.}
Write $\Pi_M\subseteq\Pi$ for the manipulable clusters and $\mu^\star(\mathbf{X}_s)=\min_{\mathbf{x}_s\in D(\mathbf{X}_s)}\E[Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s=\mathbf{x}_s)]$. The paper is organized around
\begin{gather*}
U_\Pi(\mathcal{S}):={\textstyle\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{S}}}C,\qquad
\Qpi(\Gg):=(\Pi,\Gq),\\
\Upi:=\bigl\{U_\Pi(\mathcal{S}) : \emptyset\neq\mathcal{S}\subseteq\Pi_M\bigr\},\\
V(\Pi):=\min_{A\in\Upi}\mu^\star(A),\qquad
\Delta(\Pi):=V(\Pi)-V(\Pi_\bot),
\end{gather*}
where $\Pi_\bot$ is the all-singleton partition: the quotient input, the cluster-union interventions, the best value attainable from them, and the gap coarsening opens. Since $\Upi\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\Pi_\bot)$, $\Delta(\Pi)\ge 0$ (Prop.~\ref{prop:price}).

\paragraph{Evaluation metrics.}
We report the best value found $y^{\mathrm{best}}_T$ and the \emph{empirical} incumbent regret $\hat r_t=|y^{\mathrm{best}}_t-y^\star|$ with $\hat R_T=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\hat r_t$, computed from observed incumbents (recorded with noise $\sigma_Y$), an empirical proxy for the incumbent mean regret of \S\ref{sec:theory-price}. Behavioral robustness is scored by \emph{paired-by-seed} differences between a misspecified and a correct run of the same method (\S\ref{sec:exp}).

\section{What coarsening buys}
\label{sec:theory}

An optimizer meeting the contract accepts $\Qpi(\Gg)=(\Pi,\Gq)$ in place of a fine graph: every graph-dependent component (search space, identification formulas, priors, surrogate architecture, selection rules) must be a function of that pair. The guarantee is input equivalence. It protects behavior against graph errors the quotient cannot see. Solution quality rests on the quotient itself being correct. Throughout, $\Pi,\Pi'$ are manipulable-only partitions, and $\Pi'\preceq\Pi$ ($\Pi'$ refines $\Pi$) iff every cluster of $\Pi'$ is contained in a cluster of $\Pi$ \citep{madaleno2026a}.

\subsection{What is protected?}
\label{sec:theory-protected}

\begin{theorem}[Quotient invariance is method-agnostic]
\label{thm:contract}
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a causal Bayesian optimizer that factors through the quotient: for some $\bar{\mathcal{A}}$ and every admissible hypothesis $\Gg$ (acyclic, with valid quotient under $\Pi$),
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{A}(\Gg,\mathcal{D},\omega,\eta)=\bar{\mathcal{A}}\bigl(\Qpi(\Gg),\mathcal{D},\omega,\eta\bigr)
\end{equation*}
for all data $\mathcal{D}$, seeds $\omega$, and hyperparameters $\eta$. Then for admissible $\Gg,\Gg'$,
\begin{equation*}
\Qpi(\Gg)=\Qpi(\Gg')\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \tau_{\mathcal{A}}(\Gg)=\tau_{\mathcal{A}}(\Gg'),
\end{equation*}
where $\tau_{\mathcal{A}}(\Gg)$ is the full trajectory under hypothesis $\Gg$. (Proof: App.~\ref{app:proofs}.)
\end{theorem}

Within-cluster edits (insertion, deletion, or reversal of directed or bidirected edges) and inter-cluster edits redundant in the quotient satisfy the premise. A quotient-visible edge is not protected (\S\ref{sec:exp-a}). The theorem covers both routes by which a wrong graph reaches an optimizer,
\begin{equation*}
\Gg\ \longmapsto\ \bigl(\mathcal{E}(\Gg),\ \{m_s^{\Gg}\}_{s\in\mathcal{E}(\Gg)}\bigr)\ \longmapsto\ \tau,
\end{equation*}
where $\mathcal{E}$ is the exploration set and $m_s$ the prior. A corrupted prior is overridden by interventional data while the arm survives. A removed arm is never played again. Neither map references a quotient-invisible edge.

\subsection{What does protection cost?}
\label{sec:theory-price}

\begin{proposition}[Validity is not refinement-monotone]
\label{prop:refinement_validity}
There exist a valid $\Pi$ and a refinement $\Pi'\preceq\Pi$ with $\Gq$ acyclic and $\Gg^{\Pi'}$ cyclic. Validity must be re-verified after each refinement (it remains decidable from cluster-level structure alone).
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[Refinement: values persist, membership need not]
\label{prop:refinement}
Let $\Pi'\preceq\Pi$, both valid. Every $\mathbf{X}_s\in\ESpi$ that is again a MIS of $\Gg^{\Pi'}$ lies in $\ES^{\Pi'}$ with the same identification functional. Membership itself is not refinement-monotone: a coarse cluster-union arm need not be minimal at the finer partition (App.~\ref{app:proofs}).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Price of coarsening]
\label{prop:price}
$V(\Pi)$ is attained on the exploration set \QCBO{} actually searches (Eq.~\ref{eq:ccbo_exploration}, Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}):
\begin{equation*}
\min_{A\in\ESpi}\mu^\star(A)\;=\;\min_{A\in\Upi}\mu^\star(A)\;=\;V(\Pi).
\end{equation*}
For any valid $\Pi'\preceq\Pi$,
\begin{equation*}
\Upi\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\Pi'),\qquad 0\le\Delta(\Pi')\le\Delta(\Pi),
\end{equation*}
and $\Delta(\Pi)=0$ iff some optimizer of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cbo_obj} lies in $\Upi$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Sufficient conditions for zero coarsening gap]
\label{prop:free}
Assume a semi-Markovian SCM. For a fine intervention $A\subseteq\mathbf{X}$ with value $a\in D(A)$, let
\begin{equation*}
\clpi(A):=\bigcup_{\substack{C\in\Pi\\ C\cap A\neq\emptyset}}C,\qquad B:=\clpi(A)\setminus A,
\end{equation*}
and let $B_a$ be the joint natural response of $B$ under $\doo(A=a)$. If, for every such $A$ and $a$,
\begin{align*}
&\text{(i)}\ \operatorname{supp}(B_a)\subseteq D_B(a):=\{b:(a,b)\in D(\clpi(A))\},\\
&\text{(ii)}\ B_a\perp Y_{a,b}\quad\text{for all } b\in D_B(a),
\end{align*}
then $V(\Pi)=V(\Pi_\bot)$ and $\Delta(\Pi)=0$. (Proof: App.~\ref{app:proofs}. The mixture step is $\E[Y_a]=\int\E[Y_{a,b}]\,dP(B_a=b)\ge\inf_{b\in D_B(a)}\E[Y_{a,b}]$.)
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Graph-level conditions]
\label{cor:free}
In a semi-Markovian SCM, if every intervention domain contains the support of the corresponding natural responses and confounding is cluster-contained (every backdoor path from a cluster member to $Y$ passes through another member of the same cluster), then (i) and (ii) hold, so $\Delta(\Pi)=0$ for every valid $\Pi$ satisfying these conditions.
\end{corollary}

Within this model class the contrapositive reads
\begin{equation*}
\Delta(\Pi)>0\ \Longrightarrow\
\begin{array}[t]{l}
\text{support failure, or}\\
\text{confounding escaping a cluster.}
\end{array}
\end{equation*}
Coarsening need not sacrifice the optimal attainable value: a positive action-space gap can arise from restricted intervention domains or from dependence that a hard whole-cluster intervention destroys, and both premises are tight (App.~\ref{app:proofs}). Even when $\Delta(\Pi)=0$, coarsening may still affect finite-sample regret, optimization dimension, and intervention cost.

\paragraph{Refining the partition: Hierarchical \QCBO{} (\HQCBO{}).}
\label{sec:refine}
The practitioner may declare a \emph{hierarchy} $H$: per cluster, an optional sub-partition (default: singletons). No new graph is supplied. \HQCBO{} runs Algorithm~\ref{alg:ccbo} at $\Pi$ and, when the incumbent plateaus (trigger parameters in \S\ref{sec:exp}), splits the incumbent arm's clusters via $H$, refusing any split that fails the validity re-check (Prop.~\ref{prop:refinement_validity}). On acceptance the exploration set is rebuilt and unioned with every explored arm, data carry over keyed by arm (Lemma~\ref{prop:refinement}), and only new arms receive the standard $n_0$ initialization points (App.~\ref{app:seam}). We evaluate a single refinement step. This is a value-guided coarse-to-fine descent of the partition lattice, stopping at the coarsest visited partition whose cluster-level MIS contains a value-optimal arm.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-partition regret and its removal by refinement]
\label{prop:regret}
Let $\hat a_t$ be the incumbent intervention after trial $t$ and $\mu(\hat a_t)$ its true mean value. Define
\begin{equation*}
r_t:=\mu(\hat a_t)-y^\star\ \ge 0,\qquad R_T:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}r_t.
\end{equation*}
Since $y^\star=V(\Pi_\bot)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}) and a fixed partition plays only arms in $\Upi$,
\begin{equation*}
r_t\ge\Delta(\Pi)\ \ \forall t,\qquad \Delta(\Pi)>0\ \Longrightarrow\ R_T\ge T\,\Delta(\Pi).
\end{equation*}
If refinement reaches a partition $\Pi_t$ with $\Delta(\Pi_t)=0$ and the base optimizer's incumbent is consistent on the finite arm set, then
\begin{equation*}
r_t\to 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{R_T}{T}\to 0.
\end{equation*}
\end{proposition}

The gap is a property of the action space, so no amount of data lowers $r_t$ below $\Delta(\Pi)$. Refinement corrects it by changing the action space itself. We claim no convergence rate and report post-trigger behavior empirically (\S\ref{sec:exp-c}). The trigger never observes $y^\star$, so it also fires when $\Delta(\Pi)=0$. There refinement costs only the new arms' initialization.

\begin{corollary}[Phase-scoped invariance]
\label{cor:phase}
Let $\Pi_0\succeq\Pi_1\succeq\cdots$ be the partitions \HQCBO{} visits under hierarchy $H$. For hypotheses $\Gg,\Gg'$,
\begin{equation*}
\Gg^{\Pi_j}=(\Gg')^{\Pi_j}\ \ \forall j\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \tau_H(\Gg)=\tau_H(\Gg').
\end{equation*}
Splitting a cluster exposes its internal edges at later phases. Anything confined to a never-split cluster stays protected across the whole run. (Proof: App.~\ref{app:proofs}.)
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Why not both for free?}
\label{sec:theory-exposure}
Could a quotient method keep its invariance \emph{and} evaluate sub-cluster interventions when they matter? No: sub-cluster behavior is provably not a function of quotient-level inputs. Prop.~\ref{prop:qexposure} (\S\ref{sec:qmcbo}) exhibits two SCMs that agree on every quotient-level input yet disagree on a sub-cluster intervention, so no method whose inputs factor through $(\Pi,\Gq)$, arm-based, dynamic, or model-based, can distinguish them. Refinement is not a loophole but a controlled purchase: each split spends invariance exactly where it refines (Cor.~\ref{cor:phase}).

\subsection{Arm-based: quotient \CBO{} (\QCBO{})}
\label{sec:setup}
\QCBO{} uses only cluster-level structure: a partition $\Pi$ of the manipulable variables $\mathbf{X}$ and a C-DAG $\Gq$ over its clusters and the singleton nodes. $Y$ and every non-manipulable observable stay singletons, and mixing manipulable and non-manipulable variables in one cluster is disallowed, since intervening on a cluster (jointly setting all its members, $\doo(C_k=\mathbf{c}_k)$) must be executable. When the practitioner instead holds a fine-grained hypothesis graph $\Gg$, $\Gq$ is its quotient after projecting out hidden confounders \citep{leeStructuralCausalBandits2019}. \QCBO{} itself never requires the fine graph. The exploration set is \CBO{}'s own rule (Eq.~\ref{eq:mis}) applied to the quotient, the MIS of $\Gq$ over the manipulable cluster vertices $\Pi_M$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ccbo_exploration}
  \ESpi = \bigl\{\,U_\Pi(\mathcal{S}) :
    \emptyset\neq\mathcal{S}\subseteq\Pi_M,\
    \mathcal{S}\subseteq\An_{\Gq_{\bar{\mathcal{S}}}}(Y)\,\bigr\},
\end{equation}
This is $\mathrm{MIS}(\Gq)$, and $\mathrm{POMIS}(\Gq)\subseteq\mathrm{MIS}(\Gq)$, so the possibly-optimal arms are always retained. Merging manipulable variables coarsens the action space: \QCBO{} can only set a cluster jointly, never a strict subset of it. Algorithm~\ref{alg:ccbo} (App.~\ref{app:repro}) summarizes the loop.

\paragraph{Identifiability and the two-tier prior.}
\label{sec:causal_prior}
For each arm $\mathbf{X}_s\in\ESpi$ we pose the cluster-level query $P(Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s))$ on $\Gq$ and run the complete ID algorithm of \citet{shpitserIdentificationJointInterventional} (via \texttt{ananke}; \citealp{AnankeModuleCausal}). Identifiability decides the arm's \emph{prior tier}:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:two_tier_prior}
  m_s(\mathbf{x}_s) = \begin{cases}
    \hat{\E}[Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s=\mathbf{x}_s)] & \parbox[t]{8.6em}{$P(Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s))$\\ identified in $\Gq$,}\\[6pt]
    \bar{Y}_{\mathrm{obs}} & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The identifiability test is exact where it applies: ID is complete on the C-DAG, and identification from $\Gq$ is sound for every fine graph in its class \citep{CausalEffectIdentification}. A non-identifiable arm learns its response from experimental data alone. Estimator mechanics (the GP plug-in pipeline instantiating identified formulas) are in App.~\ref{app:seam}.

\begin{assumption}[Valid C-DAG; estimator boundary]
\label{ass:valid}
The directed part of $\Gq$ is acyclic, which constrains only inter-cluster structure (intra-cluster acyclicity can even be relaxed; \citealp{madalenoCoarseningLinearNonGaussian2026}), coincides with validity of the coarsening when $\Gq$ is a quotient, and is checkable on the cluster-level object. The ID test is complete, while the estimator pipeline behind Eq.~\eqref{eq:two_tier_prior} is sound but not complete: identifiable queries it misses receive the uninformative tier (in \S\ref{sec:exp} this set is empty).
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Identity recovery]
\label{prop:identity}
At the identity partition,
\begin{equation*}
\Pi=\Pi_\bot\,\Rightarrow\,\Gq=\Gg^{*},\; \ESpi=\mathrm{MIS}(\Gg^{*}),\; m_s^{\Pi}=m_s^{\mathrm{CBO}},
\end{equation*}
so \QCBO{} reduces to \CBO{}. (Proof: App.~\ref{app:proofs}.)
\end{proposition}
App.~\ref{app:seam} documents two deviations of the \CBO{} authors' shipped exploration sets from the MIS definition and the corrected sets both methods use here.

\subsection{Dynamic: quotient DCBO (\QDCBO{})}
\label{sec:qdcbo}
Dynamic \CBO{} (DCBO; \citealp{agliettiDynamicCausalBayesian2021a}) optimizes a sequence of interventions on a dynamic Bayesian network. The graph enters the per-slice exploration sets, the emission and transition estimates, and the propagation of slice $t$'s optimum into slice $t{+}1$'s priors. \QDCBO{} applies the recipe per slice. \emph{Graph:} one vertex $C_{k,t}$ per cluster per slice, emission edges quotienting within a slice and transition edges across, validity re-checked per slice (Prop.~\ref{prop:refinement_validity}). \emph{Mechanisms:} exploration sets lifted to whole-cluster patterns and emission/transition estimates replaced by joint cluster mechanisms (the coregionalization construction of \QMCBO{} below), with slice-to-slice propagation as in DCBO at cluster level. \emph{Identity:} singleton clusters reduce every component to stock DCBO.

\begin{corollary}[\QDCBO{} inherits the contract]
\label{cor:qdcbo_invariance}
Every graph-dependent component of \QDCBO{} is a function of $(\Pi,\Gq)$ applied per slice, so Theorem~\ref{thm:contract} applies, and Prop.~\ref{prop:price} applies to its per-slice reachable intervention set.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Model-based: quotient MCBO (\QMCBO{})}
\label{sec:qmcbo}
MCBO \citep{sussexMODELBASEDCAUSALBAYESIAN2023} fits one GP per \emph{mechanism}, evaluates interventions by composing these GPs along the DAG, and selects them by GP-UCB: the graph is the surrogate's architecture. \emph{Graph:} \QMCBO{} composes along the C-DAG. \emph{Mechanisms:} one \emph{joint} mechanism per cluster, $P(\mathbf{X}_C \mid \mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{pa}(C)})$, a multi-output GP whose coregionalization kernel couples the member coordinates \citep{bonillaMultitaskGaussianProcess2007}, with joint within-cluster sampling and intervention targets lifted to whole-cluster patterns. \emph{Identity:} singleton clusters reduce to MCBO's per-node GPs.

\begin{corollary}[\QMCBO{} inherits the contract]
\label{cor:qmcbo_invariance}
Every graph-dependent component of \QMCBO{} is a function of $(\Pi,\Gq)$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:contract} applies. At $\Pi=\Pi_\bot$, \QMCBO{} is exactly MCBO, and its reachable intervention set (whole-cluster do patterns) equals \QCBO{}'s cluster-level arms, so Prop.~\ref{prop:price} applies.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Soundness of cluster mechanisms]
\label{prop:qsound}
Assume causal sufficiency and a valid $\Pi$. For every union-of-clusters intervention $\mathbf{x}_S$,
\begin{equation*}
P(\mathbf{v}\mid\doo(\mathbf{x}_S))=\prod_{C\notin S}P\bigl(\mathbf{x}_C\mid\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{pa}(C)}\bigr)\Big|_{\mathbf{x}_S}.
\end{equation*}
(Proof: App.~\ref{app:proofs}.)
\end{proposition}
The mechanism must be joint: per-member single-output GPs are exact only when the members are conditionally independent given the cluster's parents.

\begin{proposition}[Exposure]
\label{prop:qexposure}
There exist SCMs $M_1,M_2$, causally sufficient and valid for the same $\Pi$, with
\begin{equation*}
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal{I}_\Pi(M_1)=\mathcal{I}_\Pi(M_2),\\
P^{M_1}(Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{x}_A))\neq P^{M_2}(Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{x}_A))
\end{gathered}
\end{equation*}
for a strict subset $A\subsetneq C$ of some cluster, where $\mathcal{I}_\Pi(M)$ collects $M$'s quotient-level inputs: the C-DAG, the joint cluster mechanisms, and every union-of-clusters interventional distribution. (Proof: App.~\ref{app:proofs}.)
\end{proposition}

No quotient-respecting method, arm-based, dynamic, or model-based, can therefore evaluate sub-cluster arms from its inputs alone (\S\ref{sec:theory-exposure}). ToyGraph realizes the arm-based face of this trade (Fig.~\ref{fig:toybow}): merging $\{X,Z\}$ imports $X$'s latent confounding with $Y$ into the single cluster arm, so $P(Y\mid\doo(C))$ is not identifiable from the quotient even though both fine queries are identifiable, and the arm runs on the uninformative prior tier (\S\ref{sec:exp-d}).

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

The experiments answer four questions on three minimal SCMs with
closed-form oracles (\textsc{MinimalBench}, App.~\ref{app:minibench}),
then check that the contract carries across optimizer families on the
field's own benchmarks. \emph{(i)} Does quotient invisibility give exact
invariance? \emph{(ii)} Can that matter for optimization quality?
\emph{(iii)} What does a too-coarse partition cost? \emph{(iv)} Does the
contract transfer to \CBO{}, DCBO, and MCBO? Each experiment reports five
items: the SCM, the perturbation, the oracle, the prediction, and the
result.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Every condition holds the true SCM, the observational data
($n_{\mathrm{obs}}=100$ per run, one shared dataset per SCM), $y^\star$,
seeds, and trials fixed, and injects the misspecification only into the
assumed structure the method reasons with (App.~\ref{app:seam}). The
methods share one estimator backend and differ only in the structure they
consume: \CBO{} identifies on the full assumed DAG over its MIS arms (by
Prop.~\ref{prop:identity} it is \QCBO{} at the finest partition), \QCBO{}
runs the same algorithm on the quotient. $30$ seeds, $T{=}50$ trials
($60$ on \textsc{MediatedChain}), $3$ initial interventional points per
arm, unit costs. \HQCBO{}'s plateau trigger fires when the incumbent
improves by less than $\delta{=}10^{-3}$ over $k{=}5$ trials, tuned once
and fixed. Behavioral robustness is scored paired-by-seed:
$\Delta_t = \mathrm{metric}(\text{misspecified},s,t) -
\mathrm{metric}(\text{correct},s,t)$, and $\sup|\Delta|$ over trials and
seeds is the invariance check. Table~\ref{tab:minimal-taxonomy}
classifies every perturbation in advance.

\subsection{Experiment A: exploration-set corruption}
\label{sec:exp-a}
\emph{SCM:} \textsc{ParallelParent} (Fig.~\ref{fig:motivating};
$\lambda{=}1$), fine MIS $\{X_1\},\{X_2\},\{X_1,X_2\}$, $y^\star=0$ at the
joint arm. The partition $C_1=\{X_1,X_2\}$ has $\Delta(\Pi)=0$.
\emph{Perturbation:} A1 deletes $X_1\to Y$ (quotient-redundant), A2 adds
the intra-cluster $X_1\to X_2$, and A3 assumes a spurious
$X_1\leftrightarrow Y$ (quotient-visible negative control).
\emph{Oracle:} under A1 the fine MIS collapses to $\{X_2\}$, whose best
reachable value is $\lambda(4+\mathrm{Var}X_1)=4.25$.
\emph{Prediction:} \CBO{} loses the optimal arm structurally under A1.
\QCBO{} is invariant under A1 and A2 but not under A3.
\emph{Result:} \CBO{} pins at $4.25$ on every seed
(Fig.~\ref{fig:minimal-misspec}a). \QCBO{}'s arm choices, intervention
values, and incumbents are identical under A0 and A1 on every seed
($\sup|\Delta|=0$, Table~\ref{tab:minimal-taxonomy}) and it reaches
$y^\star$. Under A3 the C-DAG gains $C_1\leftrightarrow Y$, the cluster
arm drops to the uninformative tier, and the trajectory moves off its A0
path on every seed. Under A2 even fine \CBO{} moves by
$\sup|\Delta|<10^{-2}$: the corrupted singleton is irrelevant to the
optimum, which motivates Experiment B's design.

\subsection{Experiment B: prior corruption without arm loss}
\label{sec:exp-b}
\emph{SCM:} \textsc{FrontDoor}: $X_1\to M\to Y$ with latent
$X_1\leftrightarrow Y$ and a \emph{narrow-well} target, $y^\star=0$ at
$\doo(M{=}1)$. The well is a small fraction of the search domain, so the
do-calculus prior's head start decides whether the optimum is sampled
immediately or found by global search (App.~\ref{app:minibench}).
\emph{Perturbation:} B1 assumes a spurious intra-cluster confounder
$X_1\leftrightarrow M$. \emph{Oracle:} the fine MIS is untouched, but
both fine arms lose identification and drop to the uninformative tier
(Eq.~\ref{eq:two_tier_prior}). \emph{Prediction:} transient damage for
fine \CBO{}, exact invariance for \QCBO{}, since the edit is
intra-cluster. \emph{Result:} fine \CBO{} under B1 is slower early
(Fig.~\ref{fig:minimal-misspec}b), paying $\Delta\hat R_{50}=6.1\pm1.0$
cumulative regret (paired, mean$\pm$s.e., $30$ seeds), yet recovers
essentially the same final value ($0.006$ vs $0.001$, against an
observational baseline of $1.64$). \QCBO{}'s trajectories, arm choices,
and intervention values are exactly identical across the two conditions
($\sup|\Delta|=0$), its single cluster arm on the uninformative tier in
both.

\subsection{Experiment C: the price of coarsening, and buying it back}
\label{sec:exp-c}
\emph{SCM:} \textsc{MediatedChain}: $X_1\to X_2\to Y$ with
$Y=(X_2-4)^2$, $X_2=2X_1$ (plus noise), and the \emph{policy box}
$D(X_2)=[-2,2]$, fine MIS $\{X_1\},\{X_2\}$ (on a chain the joint is not
minimal, Lemma~\ref{prop:refinement}). \emph{Perturbation:} none; the
treatment is the partition $C_1=\{X_1,X_2\}$ itself. \emph{Oracle:}
$V(\Pi)=4$, $y^\star=0.04$, $\Delta(\Pi)=3.96$: the fine optimum

premise (i) of Prop.~\ref{prop:free}, and the whole-cluster arm must
clamp $X_2$ into the box. \emph{Prediction:} fixed-partition cumulative
regret grows at rate $\Delta(\Pi)$ (Prop.~\ref{prop:regret}), and
refinement removes it. \emph{Result:} \CBO{} descends to $0.040$;
fixed-partition \QCBO{} pins at $4.000$ on every seed with
$\hat R_{60}=252.7\pm1.8$; \HQCBO{} triggers at trial $7.5\pm0.1$
($30/30$ seeds, no refused splits), splits $\{X_1,X_2\}$, and descends
to $0.040\pm0.000$ with $\hat R_{60}=46.7\pm2.0$, against \CBO{}'s
$10.4\pm3.0$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:minimal-refine}). Phase 1 is byte-identical
to \QCBO{}'s prefix on every seed (Cor.~\ref{cor:phase}).

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\QCBO{} occupies an unfilled cell in the \CBO{} landscape: it neither assumes the full DAG nor learns one, but takes a coarser, more reliable input and identifies on the quotient (Table~\ref{tab:related}, App.~\ref{app:fulltables}). Methods relaxing the full-graph requirement learn structure online at per-trial cost (GACBO maintains a DAG posterior \citep{mukherjeeGraphAgnosticCausal2024}, \CEO{} hedges a graph posterior through an entropy-based acquisition \citep{branchiniCausalEntropyOptimization2023}, \citet{durandCausalBayesianOptimization2025} identify $\Pa(Y)$ under a $Y$-leaf assumption) or assume a known equivalence class \citep{parkStructuralCausalBandits}. Averaging only \emph{dilutes} a wrong edge in proportion to posterior mass, whereas the quotient pays nothing per trial and is exactly invariant to any edit it does not resolve. The identification machinery we rely on is established (C-DAG do-calculus \citep{CausalEffectIdentification}, C-DMGs over ADMGs \citep{ferreiraIdentifyingMacroCausal2025}, cluster-level Markov properties \citep{wahlFoundationsCausalDiscovery2024}, relaxed admissibility \citep{RelaxingPartitionAdmissibility2025}, C-DAGs as discovery background knowledge \citep{padhClusterDagsPowerfulBackground}), but none of it performs optimization. Closest in spirit, \citet{zeitlerMFACBOMultiFidelityAbstraction2025} coarsen the \emph{outcome} as a cheap proxy \citep{poloczekMultiInformationSourceOptimization2017,beckersAbstractingCausalModels2019}. Causal abstraction relates decisions across levels of granularity, and the quotient is such a map, applied to the manipulable \emph{inputs}.

% =============================================================================
\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
Theorem~\ref{thm:contract} is structural: coarsening acts as a hard regularizer on the hypothesis space, trading resolution (Prop.~\ref{prop:price}) for immunity to a class of misspecifications. Caveats. (i) The invariance belongs to the \emph{quotient}, and the guarantee is about \emph{behavior}. A wrong quotient produces the same trajectory for the wrong problem. (ii) DCBO and MCBO cannot join the fixed-objective seam (per-environment coupling), so \QDCBO{} and \QMCBO{} are each evaluated within their own family's harness with the base method's analytic objective held fixed. (iii) Faithfulness does not transfer to the cluster level \citep{wahlFoundationsCausalDiscovery2024}. (iv) Unit intervention costs only. (v) Building the exploration set costs one MIS test and one complete-ID check per candidate cluster union, up to $2^{K}{-}1$ of each on the quotient: exponential in clusters, not in variables. (vi) \QMCBO{} assumes causal sufficiency (Prop.~\ref{prop:qsound}), as MCBO does. A rollback rule bounding the lossless-trigger tax, and discovering the hierarchy from the run's own data, are natural follow-ups.

% =============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Coarsening runs causal Bayesian optimization on a coarser, more reliable structural commitment, with provable invariance to intra-cluster misspecification, an action-space gap that is zero under the conditions of Prop.~\ref{prop:free}, and refinement to correct the gap where it is positive (Prop.~\ref{prop:regret}). The practitioner dials resolution against robustness by choosing how much structure to commit to, in any of the three families.

\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}
Notation is as in \S\ref{sec:setup} and \S\ref{sec:theory}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:contract}]
Write $\mathcal{A}$'s run as a
sequence of decisions $d_1, d_2, \dots$ (which arm/action to evaluate,
model refits, stopping). By the factoring hypothesis each $d_t$ is a deterministic function
$d_t = f_t\big(\Qpi(\Gg),\, \mathcal{D}_{t-1},\, \omega_t\big)$ of the
quotient pair, the data collected so far, and the RNG stream. Under $\Gg$ and
$\Gg'$ with $\Qpi(\Gg)=\Qpi(\Gg')$ the first arguments coincide. The objective (the true SCM) and the
seeds coincide by the fixed-objective protocol, so
$\mathcal{D}_0, \omega_1$ coincide, hence $d_1$ coincides, hence
$\mathcal{D}_1$ coincides. Induction over $t$ gives equality of the entire
trajectory. The scope claimed after the theorem follows because
the latent projection removes only latent
vertices and an edge between two observables is never an interior vertex of
a projection path, so $(\Gg')^{*}$ and $\Gg^{*}$ differ exactly by the
modified intra-cluster edges; the quotient keeps a directed (resp.\
bidirected) cluster-level edge iff some fine edge crosses two distinct
clusters, so edits confined within clusters, or redundant across them,
leave $\Qpi(\Gg)$ unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:qsound}]
Under causal sufficiency the fine SCM is Markov relative to $\Gg$ with
mutually independent exogenous noises, so for any intervention
$\doo(\mathbf{x}_S)$ on a union of clusters $S$ the fine truncated
factorization gives
$P(\mathbf{V}\mid\doo(\mathbf{x}_S))
 = \prod_{k\notin S} P\big(X_k \mid \mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{pa}_{\Gg}(k)}\big)
 \big|_{\mathbf{X}_S=\mathbf{x}_S}$.
Group the factors by the clusters of $\Pi$. For a cluster $C\not\subseteq S$,
the product of its members' factors is, by the chain rule applied in any
topological order of $\Gg$ restricted to $C$, exactly the joint conditional
$P\big(\mathbf{X}_C \mid \mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{pa}(C)}\big)$, where
$\mathrm{pa}(C)$, the union of $C$'s parent clusters, contains every
out-of-cluster variable appearing in a member's conditioning set (validity of
$\Pi$ guarantees the quotient is acyclic, so $\mathrm{pa}(C)$ is well defined
and precedes $C$ in C-DAG order). The fine factorization therefore equals the
product over clusters of the joint cluster mechanisms with the clusters of
$S$ clamped at $\mathbf{x}_S$: the cluster-level truncated factorization.
Note both uses of sufficiency: independent noises across clusters (no
bidirected edges) and the absence of unobserved common causes that would make
a member's conditional depend on variables outside
$\mathrm{pa}(C)\cup C$. Replacing
$P(\mathbf{X}_C\mid\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{pa}(C)})$ by the product of its
per-member marginals would be exact iff the members are conditionally
independent given $\mathrm{pa}(C)$, in particular when $C$ contains no
intra-cluster edges — hence the joint mechanism in the construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:qexposure}]
Take $\mathbf{X}=\{X_2,X_3\}$ as one root cluster $C$, target
$Y = X_2 + \varepsilon_Y$ its child, all noises standard normal and
independent (sufficiency holds, and any $\Pi$ clustering $\{X_2,X_3\}$ is valid).
Let $M_1$: $X_2=\varepsilon_2$, $X_3 = a X_2 + \varepsilon_3$; and $M_2$:
$X_3=\varepsilon_3'$, $X_2 = a' X_3 + \varepsilon_2'$, with
$a' = a/(1+a^2)$, $\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_3') = 1+a^2$, and
$\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_2') = 1 - a^2/(1+a^2)$. Both induce the same
zero-mean Gaussian joint on $(X_2,X_3)$, with covariance
$\begin{psmallmatrix}1 & a\\ a & 1+a^2\end{psmallmatrix}$, hence the same
joint cluster mechanism (here, the same root-cluster distribution), and the
same $P(Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{x}_C))$ for the whole-cluster intervention (both
set $X_2$ directly). But under the sub-cluster intervention $\doo(X_3=x)$:
in $M_1$, $X_2=\varepsilon_2$ is unaffected, so
$\mathbb{E}[Y\mid\doo(X_3{=}x)]=0$ for all $x$; in $M_2$,
$X_2 = a'x$, so $\mathbb{E}[Y\mid\doo(X_3{=}x)] = a'x \neq 0$ for
$x\neq 0$, $a\neq 0$. Every quotient-respecting input (the C-DAG, the
joint cluster mechanisms, all whole-cluster interventional distributions)
coincides between $M_1$ and $M_2$, yet the sub-cluster query differs.
No function of those inputs can answer it.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:identity}]
Singleton clusters make the quotient map the identity on $\Gg^{*}$, so
$\Gq=\Gg^{*}$, unions of singleton clusters are exactly the subsets of
$\mathbf{X}$, and the MIS condition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ccbo_exploration}
coincides verbatim with Eq.~\eqref{eq:mis}. The prior construction poses the
same do-calculus queries \CBO{} poses on the full graph, so priors coincide
arm-for-arm.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[MIS reduction]
\label{lem:reduction}
Let $G$ be an ADMG with manipulable vertices $M$ and target $Y$, let
$\emptyset\neq\mathbf{W}\subseteq M$, and set
$\mathbf{W}' = \mathbf{W}\cap \An(Y)$ computed in $G_{\bar{\mathbf{W}}}$.
Then (i) $P(Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{w})) =
P(Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{W}'=\mathbf{w}|_{\mathbf{W}'}))$ for every $\mathbf{w}$;
(ii) $\mathbf{W}'$ is a MIS (or empty, when no member reaches $Y$); and
(iii) consequently
$\min_{\mathbf{X}_s\in\mathrm{MIS}(G)}\mu^\star(\mathbf{X}_s)
 = \min_{\emptyset\neq\mathbf{W}\subseteq M}\mu^\star(\mathbf{W})$:
restricting the search to MIS loses no value.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) In $G_{\bar{\mathbf{W}}}$ the members of
$\mathbf{W}\setminus\mathbf{W}'$ have no directed path to $Y$, so $Y$ is
unaffected by their values under the intervention; dropping them from the
intervention changes no mechanism on any directed path into $Y$
(do-calculus rule 3). (ii) A directed path from $w'\in\mathbf{W}'$ to $Y$
in $G_{\bar{\mathbf{W}}}$ passes through no intervened vertex (their
incoming edges are cut), hence through no member of
$\mathbf{W}\supseteq\mathbf{W}'$; the same path exists in
$G_{\bar{\mathbf{W}}'}$, which only restores edges, so
$\mathbf{W}'\subseteq\An(Y)$ there. (iii) By (i) and (ii) every candidate's
value is attained by a MIS, and $\mathrm{MIS}(G)$ is a subset of the
candidates.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:refinement_validity}]
Take $\mathbf{X}=\{a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2\}$ with fine edges $a_1\to b_1$ and $b_2\to a_2$.
The coarsest partition $\Pi=\{\{a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2\}\}$ is valid: both edges are
intra-cluster, so the quotient has a single manipulable node and is acyclic. Its
refinement $\Pi'=\{\{a_1,a_2\},\{b_1,b_2\}\}$ exposes both edges as inter-cluster
($a_1\to b_1$ contributes $A\to B$ and $b_2\to a_2$ contributes $B\to A$), inducing
the 2-cycle $A\rightleftarrows B$, so $\mathcal{G}^{\Pi'}$ is cyclic and $\Pi'$ is
invalid. Decidability is immediate from the acyclicity clause of
Def.~\ref{def:coarsening}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{prop:refinement}]
(i) Membership at $\Pi'$ is the stated hypothesis (any union of
$\Pi$-clusters is a union of $\Pi'$-clusters, so the candidate exists at
$\Pi'$; being a MIS of $\Gg^{\Pi'}$ puts it in $\ES^{\Pi'}$). For the
identification functional: quotienting is compositional, $\Gq$ being the
quotient of $\mathcal{G}^{\Pi'}$ under the induced partition of
$\Pi'$-clusters, so every fine graph compatible with $\mathcal{G}^{\Pi'}$
is compatible with $\Gq$. Identifiability from $\Gq$ means a single
functional of the observational distribution equals
$\E[Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s)]$ across all SCMs whose graph is compatible with
$\Gq$ \citep{CausalEffectIdentification}. A fortiori the same functional
works across the smaller class compatible with $\mathcal{G}^{\Pi'}$.
(ii) Non-monotone membership is witnessed by ToyGraph: $X{\cup}Z$ is the
single MIS of the coarse quotient, while $\{X,Z\}$ is not a MIS at
$\Pi_\bot$ — mutilating $Z$ cuts $X\to Z$, leaving $X$ causally inert, so
$\doo(X,Z)\equiv\doo(Z)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:price}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}(iii) applied to $\Gq$,
$\min_{A\in\ESpi}\mu^\star(A)$ equals the
minimum of $\mu^\star$ over all of $\Upi$
(the value $\mu^\star$ of a cluster union is a property of the underlying
SCM, the same at every partition that can express the union), which is
$V(\Pi)$ by definition. Unions of
$\Pi$-clusters are unions of $\Pi'$-clusters and, at $\Pi_\bot$, all
non-empty subsets of $\mathbf{X}$: $\Upi\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\Pi')
\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\Pi_\bot)$. Minima over nested families are ordered,
$V(\Pi_\bot)\le V(\Pi')\le V(\Pi)$, which gives
$0\le\Delta(\Pi')\le\Delta(\Pi)$ (in particular $\Delta(\Pi)\ge 0$).
Equality
$\Delta(\Pi)=0$ holds iff the unrestricted minimum is attained by a
union of $\Pi$-clusters, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}(iii) at
$\Pi_\bot$ the unrestricted minimum is $\min$ over Eq.~\eqref{eq:cbo_obj}'s
exploration set: exactly the stated condition.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:regret}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}(iii) at $\Pi_\bot$, $y^\star=V(\Pi_\bot)$.
A fixed partition only ever executes arms in $\Upi$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:ccbo_exploration}), so the incumbent satisfies
$\mu(\hat a_t)\ge V(\Pi)$ at every $t$, hence
$r_t=\mu(\hat a_t)-y^\star\ge V(\Pi)-V(\Pi_\bot)=\Delta(\Pi)$ and
$R_T\ge T\Delta(\Pi)$. If some visited $\Pi_t$ has $\Delta(\Pi_t)=0$, its
exploration set contains an arm of value $y^\star$
(Prop.~\ref{prop:price}); consistency of the incumbent on the finite arm
set then gives $\mu(\hat a_t)\to y^\star$, i.e.\ $r_t\to 0$, and Ces\`aro
averaging gives $R_T/T\to 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:free}]
Fix a fine intervention $A\subseteq\mathbf{X}$ and $a\in D(A)$, and let
$\clpi(A)$, $B=\clpi(A)\setminus A$, and $B_a$ be as in the statement.
Since $\Pi$ partitions the manipulable variables, $\clpi(A)\in\Upi$. By
the consistency of interventions, setting $B$ to the value it would have
taken changes nothing realization by realization: $Y_a=Y_{a,B_a}$.
Premise (i) makes every realized $b$ feasible for the joint intervention
on $\clpi(A)$, and premise (ii) lets the expectation factorize into a
mixture over feasible joint interventions,
\begin{equation*}
\E[Y_a]
= \int \E[Y_{a,b}]\, dP(B_a=b)
\;\ge\; \inf_{b\in D_B(a)} \E[Y_{a,b}]
\;\ge\; \mu^\star\bigl(\clpi(A)\bigr)
\;\ge\; V(\Pi).
\end{equation*}
Minimizing the left side over $a\in D(A)$ and then over
$A\subseteq\mathbf{X}$ gives, by Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}(iii) at
$\Pi_\bot$, $V(\Pi_\bot)\ge V(\Pi)$. The reverse inequality holds because
the coarse intervention family is contained in the fine family
($\Upi\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\Pi_\bot)$, Prop.~\ref{prop:price}), so
$V(\Pi)=V(\Pi_\bot)$ and $\Delta(\Pi)=0$. This treats an arbitrary $A$,
possibly meeting several clusters, in one step: the closure and $B_a$ are
joint objects, and no per-cluster iteration is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Cor.~\ref{cor:free}]
Represent the semi-Markovian SCM with mutually independent exogenous
variables $\mathbf{U}$, every latent common cause a single shared
exogenous variable (a bidirected edge in $\Gg^{*}$). Full intervention
support gives premise (i) directly. For premise (ii): under
$\doo(A=a)$, $B_a$ is a measurable function of $\mathbf{U}_{B}$, the
exogenous variables entering the mechanisms of $B$ and of its
non-intervened ancestors. At fixed $(a,b)$, $Y_{a,b}$ is a measurable
function of $\mathbf{U}_{Y}$, the exogenous variables entering the
mechanisms of $Y$ and of its ancestors outside $\clpi(A)$ (all of
$\clpi(A)$ is intervened in this world, so no path into $Y$ passes
through it). Suppose some $U^{*}\in\mathbf{U}_{B}\cap\mathbf{U}_{Y}$.
Then $U^{*}$ has a directed path into some member of $B$ — take $b^{*}$
to be the \emph{first} $\clpi(A)$-member on that path — and a directed
path into $Y$ avoiding $\clpi(A)$ entirely. Concatenating them
tail-to-tail yields a backdoor path from $b^{*}$ to $Y$ whose interior
avoids $\clpi(A)$, hence avoids $b^{*}$'s own cluster: a backdoor path
escaping the cluster, which cluster-contained confounding excludes. So
$\mathbf{U}_{B}$ and $\mathbf{U}_{Y}$ are disjoint sets of mutually
independent exogenous variables and $B_a\perp Y_{a,b}$ for every fixed
$(a,b)$.

Both premises are tight; dropping either admits an SCM with strictly
positive gap.
\emph{Support, realized by \textsc{MediatedChain} (\S\ref{sec:exp-c}).}
The policy box restricts hard interventions on $X_2$ to $[-2,2]$ while
the mechanism $X_2=2X_1$ drives $X_2$'s natural response to $4$, the
bowl center of $Y=(X_2-4)^2$, when $X_1{=}2$ is set. The best component
$b$ lies outside the interventional domain, so $\doo(X_1{=}2)$ strictly
beats every in-box whole-cluster intervention: $\Delta(\Pi)=3.96$
(App.~\ref{app:minibench}).
\emph{Independence.} Let $C=\{A,B\}$ with latent $U$, mechanisms $B=U$
and $Y=-B\,U+(A-1)^2$, $U$ standard normal. Under $\doo(A{=}1)$ with $B$
free, $\E[Y]=-\E[U^2]=-1$; under any whole-cluster
$\doo(A{=}a,B{=}b)$, $\E[Y]=-b\,\E[U]+(a-1)^2\ge 0$. Intervening severs
the exploitable dependence between $B$ and $Y$: the backdoor path
$B\leftrightarrow Y$ escapes the cluster and the gap is $1$ despite
unconstrained domains.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Cor.~\ref{cor:phase}]
Induct over phases. Within phase $j$, every graph-dependent component
factors through $(\Pi_j,\Gg^{\Pi_j})$, and the trigger time and split
decision are functions of the trajectory and the declared hierarchy
alone. If $\Gg^{\Pi_j}=(\Gg')^{\Pi_j}$ and the phase-$j$ prefixes
coincide, Theorem~\ref{thm:contract} applied at $\Pi_j$ makes the
phase-$j$ runs identical, hence the same trigger time and the same
refined partition $\Pi_{j+1}$. Agreement at every visited $\Pi_j$
therefore gives $\tau_H(\Gg)=\tau_H(\Gg')$. For the scope claim: an edit
confined to a never-split cluster is invisible in every $\Gg^{\Pi_j}$
(the cluster is intact at each phase), hence protected across the whole
run; an edit inside a split cluster is invisible before the split but
generally visible after (its endpoints then lie in distinct clusters),
which is exactly the exposure purchased. Quotient edges between
untouched clusters are the same at every phase.
\end{proof}

\section{MinimalBench: specification and oracles}\label{app:minibench}

The three SCMs share one parameterization — Gaussian roots, manipulable
domains $[-3,3]$ (except the \textsc{MediatedChain} policy box), unit
intervention costs, target noise $\sigma_Y=0.1$, no clipping — so every arm
value is closed-form. The structural module that defines them
(\texttt{ccbo/minibench.py}) is the same one the methods, the perturbation
registry, the structural tests, and Table~\ref{tab:minimal-taxonomy}'s
emitter consume, so specification and experiment cannot drift. Observational
datasets ($5{,}000$ rows, seed $0$; every run uses the first
$n_{\mathrm{obs}}{=}100$) are produced by released generators whose built-in
oracle gates assert the Monte-Carlo arm values against the closed forms
below (tolerance $0.05$) at build time.

\paragraph{\textsc{ParallelParent} (Experiment A).}
Latent $U$; manipulable $X_1,X_2$; target $Y$:
\begin{align*}
  U = 0.4\,\epsilon_0,\quad
  X_i = U + 0.3\,\epsilon_i,\quad
  Y = \lambda(X_1-2)^2 + (X_2+2)^2 + \sigma_Y\epsilon_Y,
\end{align*}
with $\lambda=1$; the latent projection carries $X_1\leftrightarrow X_2$
($\mathrm{corr}=0.64$), and $\mathrm{Var}X_i=0.25$. Coarse partition
$C_1=\{X_1,X_2\}$. Closed forms:
$\E[Y\mid\doo(x_1,x_2)]=\lambda(x_1-2)^2+(x_2+2)^2$, so $y^\star=0$ at the
joint arm; $\E[Y\mid\doo(x_1)]=\lambda(x_1-2)^2+4.25$ (the free co-parent's
bowl plus its variance) and $\E[Y\mid\doo(x_2)]=4.25\lambda+(x_2+2)^2$.
Under A1 (delete $X_1\to Y$) the fine MIS is $\{X_2\}$ alone —
$X_1\notin\An(Y)$ in every mutilation — so the best reachable fine value is
$G(\lambda)=\lambda\,(4+0.25)=4.25\lambda$, the analytic gap of
\S\ref{sec:exp-a}. Conditions: A1 = del $X_1\to Y$ (quotient-redundant); A2
= add $X_1\to X_2$ (intra-cluster bow with the latent $X_1\leftrightarrow
X_2$: $\doo(X_1)$ loses identification, membership intact); A3 = assume
$X_1\leftrightarrow Y$ (quotient-visible: the C-DAG gains
$C_1\leftrightarrow Y$ and the cluster arm drops to the uninformative
tier; \QCBO{} keeps running).

\paragraph{\textsc{FrontDoor} (Experiment B).}
Latent $U$; manipulable $X_1,M$; target $Y$:
\begin{align*}
  U &= 0.4\,\epsilon_0,\qquad X_1 = U + 0.3\,\epsilon_1,\qquad
  M = 2X_1 + 0.2\,\epsilon_M,\\
  Y &= 2\bigl(1-e^{-(M-1)^2/(2\cdot 0.3^2)}\bigr) + U + \sigma_Y\epsilon_Y.
\end{align*}
The latent projection carries $X_1\leftrightarrow Y$. The target is a
narrow well (width $0.3$ over the $6$-wide domain): prior corruption is a
global-search phenomenon, and on a smooth quadratic the same class of
corruption measurably costs nothing, since any BO self-corrects within a
couple of trials (cf.\ condition A2 in \S\ref{sec:exp-a}). Fine MIS $=\{X_1\},\{M\}$
(chain); $\doo(M)$ is identified by backdoor through $X_1$, $\doo(X_1)$ by
the front-door functional through $M$. Gaussian smoothing gives the closed
forms: $\E[Y\mid\doo(m)]=2(1-e^{-(m-1)^2/0.18})$, so $y^\star=0$ at
$\doo(M{=}1)$; $\E[Y\mid\doo(x_1)]=2\bigl(1-\tfrac{0.3}{\sqrt{0.3^2+0.2^2}}
e^{-(2x_1-1)^2/(2(0.3^2+0.2^2))}\bigr)$, best $\approx 0.336$ at
$x_1=1/2$; observational mean $\E[Y]\approx 1.637$. The coarse partition
$\{X_1,M\}$ quotients to the bow $C_1\to Y$, $C_1\leftrightarrow Y$: the
cluster arm runs on the uninformative tier in \emph{every} condition, and
setting $M$ directly makes the partition lossless ($V(\Pi)=y^\star=0$).
Condition B1 assumes a spurious intra-cluster confounder
$X_1\leftrightarrow M$: both fine arms lose identification (the backdoor
set $\{X_1\}$ for $\doo(M)$ is invalidated by the induced collider path,
the front-door premise for $\doo(X_1)$ by the confounded
$X_1\!\to\!M$ edge), while the quotient — which drops intra-cluster
bidirected edges — is unchanged.

\paragraph{\textsc{MediatedChain} (Experiment C).}
No latents; manipulable $X_1,X_2$; target $Y$:
\begin{align*}
  X_1 = 0.5\,\epsilon_1,\qquad X_2 = 2X_1 + 0.2\,\epsilon_2,\qquad
  Y = (X_2-4)^2 + \sigma_Y\epsilon_Y,
\end{align*}
with $D(X_1)=[-3,3]$ and the \emph{policy box} $D(X_2)=[-2,2]$. Fine MIS
$=\{X_1\},\{X_2\}$: the joint is not minimal on a chain (mutilating $X_2$
severs $X_1$ from $\An(Y)$; Lemma~\ref{prop:refinement}), so the joint
intervention exists only as the quotient's cluster arm, which must clamp
$X_2$ into the box. Closed forms: $\E[Y\mid\doo(x_1)]=(2x_1-4)^2+0.04$, so
$y^\star=0.04$ at $\doo(X_1{=}2)$ — driving $X_2$'s natural response to
$4$, outside the box; $\E[Y\mid\doo(x_2)]=(x_2-4)^2$, best over the box
$=4$; hence $V(\Pi)=4$ and the price $V(\Pi)-y^\star=3.96$
(Prop.~\ref{prop:price}; premise (i) of Prop.~\ref{prop:free} fails by
construction). The declared hierarchy splits $C_1=\{X_1,X_2\}$ into
singletons; one split reaches the identity partition, whose MIS contains
the optimal $\doo(X_1)$ arm.

\paragraph{Structural tests and oracle gates.}
Dataset-free structural tests assert, per condition, the exact fine MIS,
the C-DAG signature (in)equalities, and the prior-tier flips claimed above,
and end-to-end regressions assert \QCBO{}'s trajectory invariance (arm
choices and intervention values exactly; incumbents to $10^{-8}$) under
every protected condition and its divergence under A3. The data generators
refuse to write datasets whose Monte-Carlo arm values, correlations, or
policy-box geometry contradict the closed forms.

\section{Fixed-objective protocol: per-method details}\label{app:seam}
The invariant is that every method optimizes the \emph{same} objective, the true
SCM's noise-free \texttt{intervention\_function}, and only the \emph{structure each
method reasons with} is perturbed. We realize this per method as follows.

\textbf{\QCBO{} (finest and coarse).} The objective is read from the original graph's
SEM, while the structure is supplied separately through the assumed graph name
(\texttt{CoarsenedGraph(\ldots, assumed\_graph\_name=variant)}). Swapping the assumed
graph changes the quotient, the exploration set, the identification formulas, and the
prior, but never the SEM that generates outcomes.
\emph{Prior pipeline.} An identifiable arm's prior mean instantiates the
identified formula as a pipeline of GP-based plug-ins (backdoor $\to$
front-door $\to$ $g$-computation) on the bidirected-edge expansion of
$\Gq$. A non-identifiable arm — and any identifiable query the
sound-but-incomplete pipeline cannot instantiate — shares the common
uninformative prior, the observational mean of $Y$. Because clusters are
manipulable-only and interventions assign full clusters, the cluster-level
formulas expand exactly into fine-grained regressions on observational
data.

\textbf{\CBO{} (full-DAG identification).} \CBO{} is \QCBO{}'s backend at the
identity partition (Prop.~\ref{prop:identity}): it runs the same complete
identification algorithm, only on the full DAG rather than a quotient. The
injection is the same assumed-graph swap as for \QCBO{} (the perturbed
structure reaches the exploration set and the identification formulas that build
the prior), while outcomes are always drawn from the true SEM. Its trajectory
is identical to \QCBO{}'s at the finest partition on every graph.

\emph{Exploration-set disclosure.} Both methods use the MIS of the assumed
graph (Eq.~\ref{eq:mis}), computed by the released code and verified against
hand-derived Lee--Bareinboim ground truth per benchmark (for the minimal
suite, the per-condition sets are also asserted by the structural tests of
App.~\ref{app:minibench}). The \CBO{} authors'
shipped code deviates from that definition in two places, which we correct
for both methods in the family suite: on ToyGraph it lists the non-minimal
arm $\{X,Z\}$
(mutilating $Z$ cuts $X\to Z$, so $\doo(X,Z)\equiv\doo(Z)$ and the joint arm
is redundant), and on CoralGraph it truncates the list at subsets of size
three (an ad-hoc cap; the definition yields all $31$ non-empty subsets, as
every manipulable variable keeps a directed path to $Y$ through
never-intervened non-manipulables under any mutilation).

\textbf{\HQCBO{}.} Phase 1 is the \QCBO{} configuration run by the same
code path with the same seed (the trigger is a function of the coarse
trajectory alone), so its pre-trigger prefix is byte-identical to the
\QCBO{} run — asserted per seed. The refined phase re-enters the same
backend with the split partition, the unioned exploration set, and the
carried per-arm data (\S\ref{sec:refine}); the vendored loop forces an
observation step at the start of a phase, a small bias \emph{against}
refinement that we keep.

\textbf{Objective-identity verification.} For all methods the decoupling
holds by construction: outcomes are always evaluated on the true graph's
SEM, and the perturbation enters only through the separately-supplied
assumed-graph variant (including assumed latents, as in A3 and B1). The
released data generators additionally refuse to build any dataset whose
Monte-Carlo arm values contradict the closed-form oracles
(App.~\ref{app:minibench}): feeding a method a wrong graph changes only its
causal reasoning, never the problem it is solving.

\section{Implementation and reproducibility}\label{app:repro}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\QCBO{}: Quotient Causal Bayesian Optimization}
\label{alg:ccbo}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE C-DAG $\Gq$ over the clusters of $\Pi$ (or a fine hypothesis $\Gg$ and
$\Pi$); target $Y$; number of trials $T$; observational data $\mathcal{D}^{O}$;
optional hierarchy $H$ (\S\ref{sec:refine}).
\STATE If given $\Gg$: $\Gg^*\leftarrow$ latent projection of $\Gg$;\quad
$\Gq\leftarrow$ quotient of $\Gg^*$ under $\Pi$. Check acyclicity of $\Gq$
(Assumption~\ref{ass:valid}).
\STATE Build $\ESpi \leftarrow \mathrm{MIS}(\Gq)$ via
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ccbo_exploration}.
\STATE For each $\mathbf{X}_s\in\ESpi$: run complete ID on
$P(Y\mid\doo(\mathbf{X}_s))$ and fit the prior mean $m_s$ via the two-tier
rule, Eq.~\eqref{eq:two_tier_prior}, on $\mathcal{D}^{O}$.
\STATE Collect initial interventional data $\mathcal{D}^{I}_0$.
\FOR{$t=1,\ldots,T$}
  \STATE Update GP posteriors $(m_{s,t},k_{s,t})$ for all $\mathbf{X}_s\in\ESpi$.
  \STATE $(\mathbf{X}_{s_t},\mathbf{x}_t)\leftarrow\arg\max_{s,\mathbf{x}}
  \mathrm{EI}^{s}_{t}(\mathbf{x})/Co(\mathbf{X}_s,\mathbf{x})$.
  \STATE Intervene, observe $y_t$; update $\mathcal{D}^{I}_t$.
  \STATE \textbf{if} $H$ declared \textbf{and} incumbent plateaued: split the
  incumbent's clusters via $H$, re-check validity, rebuild
  $\ESpi\,\cup$ past arms, carry $\mathcal{D}^{I}_t$ by arm, init new arms
  (\S\ref{sec:refine}).
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $(\mathbf{X}_{s_{t^\star}},\mathbf{x}_{t^\star})$ where $t^\star=\arg\min_t y_t$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

All methods share one surrogate/acquisition backend (the vendored \CBO{} loop), so
comparisons differ only in the assumed structure. \textbf{Surrogate.} Each
exploration-set arm carries a \textsc{GPy} \texttt{GPRegression} model with an RBF
kernel (signal variance and lengthscale initialized to $1$, ARD off) and observation
noise fixed near zero ($10^{-10}$, matching the deterministic mechanisms). The causal
prior supplies the do-calculus estimate as the GP \emph{mean function}, so
$\Delta$-metrics isolate the effect of a wrong prior/exploration set from surrogate
noise. \textbf{Acquisition.} Causal expected improvement \citep{garnettBayesianOptimization2023}, maximized
over the box-constrained intervention domain by gradient-based L-BFGS.
\textbf{Protocol (minimal suite).} Unit per-variable intervention costs,
$3$ initial interventional points per arm, $T{=}50$ trials ($60$ on
\textsc{MediatedChain}), $30$ seeds, $n_{\mathrm{obs}}{=}100$ from one
shared dataset per SCM; the family suite follows each base paper's own
protocol (\S\ref{sec:exp-d}). Every run additionally logs the selected arm
and intervention values per trial, so invariance is checked on
\emph{decisions}, not only on incumbent values.
\textbf{Invariance tolerance.} Arm-choice and intervention-value sequences
are compared exactly; incumbent trajectories at $10^{-8}$ (GP-solver
floating point). On every protected condition of
Table~\ref{tab:minimal-taxonomy} the sequences are identical and
$\sup|\Delta|=0$; the quotient-visible control moves trajectories on every
seed.
\textbf{Artifacts.} The MinimalBench structural module
(\texttt{ccbo/minibench.py}), its data generators with built-in oracle
gates, the dataset-free structural tests and end-to-end invariance
regressions, the suite runner (\texttt{scripts/run\_minimal\_suite.py}),
the native two-phase \HQCBO{} (\texttt{ccbo/minimal\_suite.py}), and every
table/figure emitter are released, so all reported numbers regenerate from
the released SCMs and seeds.
