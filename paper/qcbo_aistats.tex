\begin{abstract}
\guide{Four moves, ~150 words. (1) \emph{Setup+pain:} CBO needs the full DAG;
a single wrong edge silently corrupts it — either deleting the optimal arm
(unrecoverable) or biasing the prior (slow). (2) \emph{Idea:} \QCBO{} optimizes
over a \emph{partition} of the manipulable variables via the quotient graph and
never looks inside a cluster; intra-cluster misspecification is \emph{provably
invisible}. (3) \emph{Evidence:} on a purpose-built 10-variable SCM we run the
full field (\CBO/\CEO/CoCaBO) under a taxonomy of misspecifications with the
\emph{objective held fixed} — every causal baseline degrades, gating ones
permanently, while \QCBO-coarse is byte-identical ($\Delta=0$); the protection
is \emph{exactly} scoped — the same edge placed across clusters is catastrophic.
(4) \emph{Bonus:} coarsening is also more sample-efficient on the standardized
\textsc{CausalBO} benchmark. End with the one-line thesis: robustness at the
level of structural knowledge practitioners actually have.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
\story{This is a robustness-first paper. By the end of page~1 the reader must
believe: (i) the full-DAG assumption is a real liability, (ii) practitioners
know \emph{groups} not edges, and (iii) coarsening turns that weaker knowledge
into provable robustness — \emph{plus} a sample-efficiency win.}

\guide{Paragraph 1 — the hook. CBO unifies ``intervene, don't just observe'' with
``use the graph for a prior,'' but inherits a brittle input: the \emph{entire}
DAG over all observables, every edge and latent, specified correctly. State that
a single misspecified edge is not a benign approximation — it can silently and
\emph{permanently} cost you the optimum.}

\guide{Paragraph 2 — the knowledge mismatch. Practitioners reliably know
\emph{clusters} (a clinician groups co-prescribed drugs as one ``treatment''
lever; a reef ecologist groups nutrient/chemistry vs.\ temperature/depth) but
\emph{not} the within-group edges. The two ends of the spectrum (full DAG;
learn-the-graph-online at heavy per-trial cost) are served; the practical middle
— cluster-level knowledge — has identification theory but no optimization method.}

\guide{Paragraph 3 — the idea + why it is robust. \QCBO{}: supply a partition
$\pi$ of the manipulable variables; build the quotient graph $\Gq$; restrict to
cluster-level interventions identifiable from $\Gq$; use the identified
cluster-level do-effects as GP prior means. The one-sentence insight: the
construction \emph{never looks inside a cluster}, so any edge error confined to a
cluster cannot be represented, let alone hurt the optimizer.}

\guide{Paragraph 4 — name the two damage channels up front (this is the lens for
the whole paper): a wrong edge corrupts either (a) the \emph{exploration set} via
identifiability gating $\Rightarrow$ deletes an arm $\Rightarrow$ \emph{unrecoverable};
or (b) the \emph{prior} $\Rightarrow$ bias $\Rightarrow$ recoverable by the BO loop.
Foreshadow: methods differ in which channel bites them.}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\guide{Three bullets, ordered by emphasis. Make (1) clearly the headline.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Robustness via coarsening (primary).}
  \guide{An intra-cluster \emph{invariance} property (every quantity \QCBO{}
  computes is invariant to arbitrary within-cluster edge errors) \emph{and} the
  full-field empirical demonstration that the field degrades where \QCBO-coarse
  does not. Promise the exact \emph{scope}: protection holds iff the perturbed
  edge is invisible in the quotient.}
  \item \textbf{Sample efficiency (secondary).}
  \guide{Coarsening concentrates a fixed budget on fewer, identifiable
  cluster-level arms; a price-of-coarsening characterization says when this is
  lossless. On the standardized benchmark \QCBO-coarse attains higher GAP at
  comparable final $Y$.}
  \item \textbf{Method + theory (enabling).}
  \guide{\QCBO{} formalized: complete C-DAG identification, gated exploration
  set, two-tier causal GP prior, Algorithm~\ref{alg:qcbo}; short self-contained
  proofs.}
\end{itemize}

\section{What coarsening buys}
\label{sec:theory}
\story{This section earns the word ``provably'' in the abstract. Lead with the
robustness theorem (Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}); everything else supports it.
Keep statements in-text, proofs in the appendix.}

\begin{proposition}[Identity recovers CBO]\label{prop:identity}
\guide{At the finest partition (all singletons) $\Gq=$ the full DAG and \QCBO{}
reduces to \CBO{} with the identifiability-gated exploration set. This anchors
\QCBO-finest as the ``full-knowledge'' reference and means any robustness gap
between finest and coarse is attributable to \emph{coarsening}, not to a different
implementation.}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Intra-cluster invariance — the robustness theorem]\label{prop:invariance}
\guide{State it as the headline: if $G'$ differs from $G$ only by inserting/
deleting/reversing edges whose endpoints lie \emph{within a single cluster} (with
$G'$ acyclic), then $(G')^\pi=G^\pi$, hence the exploration set, every
identification formula, every prior, and the entire \QCBO{} trajectory are
\emph{identical}. Proof idea (one line): projection touches only latents; the
quotient keeps a cluster edge iff some fine edge crosses clusters, so intra-cluster
edits leave $\Gq$ fixed.}
\guide{Then state the \textbf{refined scope} that the experiments establish:
protection holds iff the perturbed edge is \emph{quotient-invisible} — intra-cluster
\emph{or} inter-cluster-but-redundant (a parallel cluster edge survives). And the
sharp negative: a quotient-\emph{visible} edge can change the trajectory; an
inter-cluster bow makes a cluster arm non-identifiable and is catastrophic. This
is what makes the claim honest rather than magical.}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Damage taxonomy — the experimental lens]\label{rem:taxonomy}
\guide{Define Tier-1 (prior bias, recoverable: wrong edge keeps the arm but
mis-primes it; the BO loop heals it) vs Tier-2 (arm deletion, unrecoverable: a wrong
edge flips an identifiability verdict and removes the optimal target — no data can
restore a missing arm). Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance} immunizes against both when the
misspecification is quotient-invisible. This taxonomy organizes \S\ref{sec:exp}.}
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Price of coarsening + losslessness]\label{prop:price}
\guide{The attainable optimum is monotone along the coarsening lattice, with an
\emph{exact} losslessness condition: coarsening costs nothing iff some optimizer is
a union of clusters. This is the bridge to the sample-efficiency claim
(\S\ref{sec:exp-eff}) and the reason ClusterBench10 is built so the optimum is a
union of clusters.}
\end{proposition}

\guide{Mention Props.~3--4 (refinement: validity is \emph{not} refinement-monotone;
exploration/identifiability \emph{are} monotone under valid refinement) in one
sentence and push full statements/proofs to the appendix — they are not on the
robustness critical path.}

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}
\story{The heart of the paper. Arc: (5.1) make the comparison \emph{fair} (fixed
objective, perturbed structure) — this is the methodological crux reviewers will
probe; (5.2) the headline — the whole field degrades, \QCBO-coarse does not;
(5.3) characterize \emph{exactly} which misspecifications are protected, and show
\QCBO-coarse itself breaking when the edge is not abstracted away; (5.4) the
sample-efficiency bonus.}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}
\guide{Three things, tightly. (a) \textbf{ClusterBench10}: a purpose-built
10-variable SCM — manipulable $X_1\!\dots\!X_5$, non-manipulable mediators
$M_1,M_2,M_3,Z$, target $Y$; latents $U_{12}\!\to\!\{X_1,X_2\}$ (intra-cluster) and
$U_{15}\!\to\!\{X_1,X_5\}$ (cross-cluster); coarse partition $C_1{=}\{X_1,X_2,X_3\}$,
$C_2{=}\{X_4,X_5\}$. State the oracle-verified design facts: $\do(X_1)$ is the
strongest singleton, the global optimum $\do(\text{all}){=}\do(C_1\!\cup\!C_2)$ is a
union of clusters (so the coarse partition is \emph{lossless} — robustness is not
confounded by a price), and $\mathrm{corr}(X_1,X_2)\approx0.70$. Full spec in
Appendix~\ref{app:scm}.}
\guide{(b) \textbf{The fair-comparison seam} (emphasize!): every method optimizes
the \emph{identical} objective — same true SCM, same observational data, same
$y^\ast$, seeds, budget — and we perturb \emph{only the structure each method
reasons with} (its adjacency/candidate graph), never the objective. We verify the
objective is byte-identical under every perturbation. Without this, a head-to-head
under misspecification is not interpretable; with it, any performance change is
attributable purely to (mis)structure.}
\guide{(c) \textbf{Methods + metric}: \BO{} (non-causal control), \CBO{} (non-gating
causal prior), \CEO{} (gating, graph-uncertainty competitor), CoCaBO, and
\QCBO-finest/\QCBO-coarse. Metric: paired-by-seed
$\Delta = \text{metric}(\text{misspecified},s) - \text{metric}(\text{correct},s)$
on final $Y$ and GAP. Note DCBO/MCBO are out of scope (they hardcode the
environment / couple surrogate structure to the objective, so ``wrong structure,
same objective'' is not separable) — say this once, here or in Limitations.}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\story{Position by \emph{how each handles structural uncertainty}, so \QCBO{}'s
``abstract it away'' reads as a distinct third option.}
\guide{Three clusters of work: (1) the \CBO{} family (assumes the full DAG — the
fragility we address). (2) Graph uncertainty — esp.\ \CEO{}, which \emph{averages
over candidate DAGs} at per-trial cost; contrast: averaging hedges over graphs vs
\QCBO{} \emph{commits} to a coarser-but-correct quotient, and show empirically
graph-averaging only partially protects. (3) Cluster causal diagrams / C-DAG
identification (gives us the machinery but no optimizer) and robust BO (robust to
\emph{observation} noise, not \emph{structural} error). One sentence each.}

% =============================================================================
\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\story{Convert anticipated objections into the paper's framing — honesty is a
strength here.}
\guide{Hit: (i) \emph{Honest gating-fragility}: \QCBO-\emph{finest} is as fragile as
the gating field — the win is \emph{coarsening}, not the implementation. (ii) \BO{}
is trivially robust but pays in absolute performance (robustness$\ne$ignoring
structure). (iii) The gate commits to the assumed structure; an un-gated variant
trades arm-deletion for exploration overhead — worth comparing. (iv) DCBO/MCBO
excluded (structure coupled to objective / hardcoded environments). (v)
Faithfulness does not transfer to the cluster level. (vi) Unit intervention costs;
cost-asymmetry changes where coarsening pays.}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\guide{Two sentences: \QCBO{} makes causal BO usable at the level of structural
knowledge practitioners actually have, with provable + empirical robustness to
within-cluster misspecification and a sample-efficiency bonus; the practitioner
chooses where to sit on the resolution/robustness dial by choosing the partition.}
