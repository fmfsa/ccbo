# Scientific experiment redesign

Protocol decision record, 16 September 2026. This reconciles three independent audits of scientific validity, matched feedback, and model-based baselines. It specifies the analyses before final evaluation runs. The final machine-readable configuration, source revisions, environment locks, and this document must be hashed together before launch. This document does not claim that all implementations or runs are complete.

## What the revised experiments will establish

Two primary tracks are authorized: corrected MCBO/QMCBO comparisons, and matched **noisy true-SCM feedback** for the controlled examples. Exact population-response experiments remain valid constructed mechanism evidence, separately labeled. Their existence does not validate comparisons against a method trained on noisy measurements.

The apparent disagreement between the independent reports is resolved by separating questions. Exact-response Bayesian optimization is a legitimate idealized information regime; using the same oracle for queried training labels and scoring is permissible when declared. However, comparing it directly with noisy CEO changes the information supplied to learners, and using an inaccessible population score to choose a noisy learner's incumbent changes the decision problem. The primary comparison therefore uses genuine noisy measurements and an external scorer. No 750-run exact-response rerun is required before its gates.

The examples were deliberately constructed to exhibit mechanisms, and the narrow FrontDoor well and small initialization were selected after exploratory pilots. We will preserve those pilots and historical results, freeze the current SCMs/domains, and describe new runs as corrected replication on constructed examples. New seeds do not convert selected benchmarks into evidence of average benefit across real problems.

The principal hypotheses are:

1. **Protected-input invariance:** coupled QCBO runs with identical quotients, data and semantic random streams have identical actions, measurements, recommendations and refinement events. This is an implementation contract, not an average-performance hypothesis.
2. **Arm removal:** PP A1 restricts fine CBO to X2, forcing the population value of every eligible recommendation to at least 4.25, while the quotient preserves the globally optimal joint arm. Noise cannot remove this feasible-action floor.
3. **Prior information:** FD B1 keeps the same fine arms as B0 but removes their identified priors. The mean paired effect on recommendation quality will be estimated; positive damage is not an acceptance condition. A forced-fallback control is needed before attributing every difference solely to prior replacement.
4. **Action restriction and refinement:** fixed coarse MC recommendations have population value at least 4, versus fine optimum .04. The hierarchy exposes the useful X1 arm after a split; reaching .04 within a finite budget is an empirical outcome, not a required success criterion.
5. **Model-based comparison:** after correcting mapping, scalar fitting, random-stream and scoring defects symmetrically, differences between fine/full-actions, fine/coarse-actions and quotient/coarse-actions describe action-menu and modeling effects. They do not by themselves measure a structural coarsening price.

## Controlled noisy protocol

Keep the three existing SCM equations, domains, partitions, perturbations, causal-prior settings and hierarchy. In particular, retain FrontDoor width .3 and MC's X2 policy box [-2,2]. Generate exactly 100 fresh observational rows per replicate from the **true** SCM using a separate named random stream. Every method and graph condition in that replicate receives the same rows; latent variables remain hidden. No later observation actions are allowed.

Each purchased intervention produces one genuine full observable row from the true SCM. Scalar optimizers may consume only its Y coordinate; CEO may use its full row as graph evidence. No method receives a second measurement or a population response disguised as its bookkeeping channel. Graph misspecification changes only the optimizer's supplied information, never the environment, domains or data generation.

Use three initial points per native arm. Draw a canonical arm-keyed table of uniform intervention values and separately keyed measurement disturbances. Every common arm has bitwise-identical initialization across methods and conditions, independent of enumeration order or removal of other arms. A method receives only its permitted arms and pays for every row it receives. New refinement arms receive separately keyed, paid initialization at split time; their outcomes are not exposed earlier.

The primary recommendation rule is the executed point with the smallest **measured** Y, with deterministic tie-breaking by canonical arm and execution index. This rule is deliberately simple and implementable; true recommendation quality can worsen because of measurement noise. Serialize its action ID before external scoring. Report an oracle-best-visited population value only as a separate diagnostic bound. Never take a running minimum of population scores and call that a noisy learner's recommendation. A posterior-mean recommendation sensitivity is permitted only if specified and implemented for all relevant methods before the final matrix starts; it must not replace the primary after observing results.

The external scorer evaluates the analytical population mean at the serialized recommendation. It cannot set an acquisition baseline, GP label, graph posterior, split trigger or recommendation. The scorer must consume no learner/environment random state. Noisy CBO/BO require an explicitly implemented positive-noise likelihood; the current exact-response fixed variance 1e-10 is unsuitable as their primary noisy model. Before final launch, record the actual likelihood bounds, initialization, target transformation and optimizer policy in the frozen manifest. Exact mode retains its separate near-noiseless likelihood. Distinct native model classes may retain different inductive assumptions, but all receive the same purchased measurement channel.

The primary resource budget is total intervention cost, including initialization and split purchases: **100 for PP/FD; 120 for MC**, at one unit per manipulated variable per measured row. Only affordable arms may be proposed. Stop when no allowed action is affordable; do not obtain or reveal an over-budget response. A split's complete declared design must be affordable before activation. Log unspent budget and refused splits. The primary comparison matches experimental resources, not wall-clock compute; log acquisition evaluations and runtime separately.

Primary metric: population recommendation gap at the fixed final budget, using y*=0 for PP/FD and .04 for MC. Secondary metric: the sum of right-continuous recommendation gaps at integer cost checkpoints **12 through B**, with no interpolation using future observations. Cost 12 is the largest native initialization cost among these methods. Show the earlier initialization events separately rather than inventing recommendations before they are earned. Report raw objective, total spent cost, measured-row count, initialization/split cost, and refinement time. Intervention-index cumulative recommendation regret is a secondary metric, distinct from cost-integrated regret and from regret of the executed actions.

Semantic random streams separate observational data, initialization levels, initialization measurements, online environment disturbances, algorithm/fit randomness, and scoring. Couple protected pairs by intervention index and shared initial-arm row, so equal actions receive exactly equal measurements. Algorithms may see different responses after their actions diverge. A common integer seed alone does not provide this contract.

## Exact attribution conditions

The no-prior RBF model and the nonidentified observational fallback are different priors. CBONP is therefore not an exact fallback control. For a genuine B0-versus-B1 prior-only check, hold fixed the arm list/order `{X1},{M}`, common initial measurements, revealed observations, GP likelihood/kernel/hyperparameter policy, acquisition optimizer, costs, random streams, and recommendation rule. In the B0 control force **both mean and variance functions** to the same observational mean/variance fallback that B1 uses. Any unused graph-dependent work must not perturb the algorithm stream.

Under those conditions, B0-forced-fallback and B1-CBO must produce identical complete traces. If they do not, diagnose an additional implementation pathway; do not infer “prior only” from similar final regrets. Plain CBONP remains a distinct ablation of causal mean/covariance construction. The A0-versus-A1 CBONP contrast isolates the effect of the changed arm menu within that implementation, allowing different charged initialization menus while preserving shared-arm data.

Historical exact-response runs can continue to demonstrate analytic floors and invariance if their actual logs satisfy the corresponding contracts. Cost-ledger corrections or independent rescoring may be performed from complete logs without changing their learner histories. Missing action identities cannot be reconstructed from a best-value curve alone. Do not overwrite old results with corrected outputs under the same filenames.

## Nonempty action values and what the theorem correction changes

The replacement theory correctly distinguishes all-nonempty action value V from retained-MIS value W. Null-augmented equality does not imply V=W without an additional condition. Observing data is not the same as allowing a null recommendation. Apply MIS reduction only to a graph compatible with the evaluated SCM.

For the three controlled SCMs, this correction does **not** change the numerical reference values or force new experiments: PP's observational mean is 8.5 and its retained joint optimum is 0; FD's observational mean is about 1.64 and retained do(M=1) attains 0; MC's observational mean is 17.04 and retained fine do(X1=2) attains .04, versus coarse optimum 4. Their true retained optima beat the null value. In A1 the supplied wrong graph leaves only X2, whose true optimum is 4.25; the true-graph MIS theorem is not used to justify this wrong-graph restriction.

The model-based Toy simulator maximizes `exp(-z/20)-cos(z)`. At zero noise, do(X) produces z=exp(-x)>0 and hence reward at most 2. The admissible do(Z=-π) gives reward greater than 2, so the global nonempty optimum is attained by do(Z) and by the joint arm; its precise value can be found over the bounded Z domain using endpoints and stationary points. The null reward at z=1 is smaller. In PSA, with cancer probability p, the underlying target-mean derivatives are `dY/dA=.55+.02p(1-p)>0` and `dY/dS=-.60-.04p(1-p)<0`. Thus maximizing reward -Y is pointwise optimized by the joint action A=0,S=1, which dominates natural/singleton assignments for every age/BMI realization. Its value requires integration, but its optimizer is known. Both these coarse action families therefore have zero structural price under the stated domains, despite possible finite-budget performance differences.

For other family tasks, do not claim that a nonempty MIS optimizer attains the full nonempty action optimum without checking the condition. Add an analytic regression fixture for the corrected lemma's independent-root example, V=.01 versus W=1, rather than changing the global action convention. Full natural-response support is sufficient for a zero-price theorem, not a prerequisite for running the algorithm; widening MC's policy box would destroy its intended distinct question.

## Dynamic SEM correction and distinct population protocol

The original dynamic evaluator explicitly sets every disturbance to zero. Its scalar return is a **zero-disturbance response**, not a population expectation for nonlinear SCMs. Moreover, its inherited blanket machinery clamps previous targets to their estimated means. Historical trajectories therefore remain historical executions of that specific protocol; they cannot be relabeled as population-policy outcomes.

The independent exact-conditional diagnostic confirmed that both corrected-stock and quotient emission-plus-transition dispatch add two regressions of the full child response. In the declared linear Gaussian witness, actual pinned-backend execution gave mean error 7.5 and variance error 5.3125 under one common fixed blanket. A coherent union-parent implementation subsequently reduced that witness's maximum error to 3.55×10^-15. The first four real-GP stat smoke units passed singleton and protected-quotient trace identity. Those gates established a bounded implementation result; subsequent residual-law and population-objective changes require their own current-version gates.

The new coherent estimator fits each cluster once on the **union of contemporaneous and lagged parents**. Root clusters use their empirical joint marginal. Singleton conditionals retain the shared scalar GP policy. Nonroot multivariate conditionals use a joint latent GP plus an explicitly modeled full residual covariance: aligned **leave-one-joint-row-out** residual covariance, 10% shrinkage toward its diagonal, and a small positive-definiteness floor. For each original row, with its task indices I, compute the held-out residual as inverse(Kinv[I,I]) times alpha[I]; scalar-coordinate leave-one-out would improperly retain peer outputs from that row. The stock diagonal likelihood is excluded from predictive covariance before adding that residual term. This is a residual predictive approximation, not a proof of recovered structural noise: leave-one-out residuals also contain mean-estimation error and misspecification, so uncertainty may be conservative.

Common-random-number ancestral integration propagates sampled parent values through nonlinear conditional models. The final target uses mean E[μ(parent)] and variance E[v(parent)]+Var[μ(parent)]. This variance is **total outcome-predictive variance**, not uncertainty in the expected response and not a Monte Carlo standard error. Defaults use 256 integration particles. Partial interventions on a multivariate cluster are rejected; empirical marginals, Gaussian conditionals, extrapolation and finite integration remain disclosed modeling limitations. The same implementation at singleton resolution supports an executable identity check. `--action-menu coarse` supplies a fine-model/coarse-menu control.

The new population objective is separately selected with `--mechanism-protocol coherent --objective-protocol population-mc`. At time t it evaluates E[Y_t | do(the previously committed manipulator actions and the current candidate)]. **Only explicitly selected X/Z assignments are clamped**; every free past variable, including Y, is re-simulated through the true SCM. No previous target is fixed to an estimated mean, and no unselected mediator is silently inserted into the intervention history. This evaluates a committed intervention-prefix sequence, not a response conditioned on one realized past trajectory.

Training receives a deterministic common-random-number Monte Carlo approximation using **2,048 true-SCM prefix draws** per query. It is a controlled population-oracle approximation, distinct from the primary controlled suite's one-row noisy feedback. The learner chooses its recommendation using those purchased training values. Only after all decisions are complete, an independent named stream with **100,000 draws** scores the serialized recommendations and committed prefixes, with Monte Carlo standard errors. Scoring precision and scoring results cannot influence subsequent decisions. Log action/history, response precision, measured oracle estimate, recommendation ID, external score and standard error separately. Do not enforce monotonicity of external recommendation values.

The original nonstationary task is excluded from this new primary population comparison. Its change-point equation divides by X_(t−1); an unclamped Gaussian denominator gives reciprocal-normal tails that can defeat integrability, and a zero-disturbance zero denominator can be undefined. Its supplied graph also omits the actual cross-lag X_(t−1)→Z_t at the change and Z_(t−1)→Y_t afterward. These are mathematical/model-definition issues, not disappointing optimization outcomes.

A **separately named `nonstat_regularized` companion** is authorized, with its formula fixed before viewing results: at the change point set Z_t=−X_t/sqrt(1+X_(t−1)^2)+Z_(t−1)+ε_Z; retain the other equations and add their true cross-lag graph edges. The denominator is at least one, so |Z_t|≤|X_t|+|Z_(t−1)|+|ε_Z| at the change. The remaining target mechanisms have at most linear growth in their inputs; the initial square-root mechanism is bounded by a constant plus |Z_0|. Finite second moments therefore propagate over a finite Gaussian-driven horizon. This is a new benchmark and requires its own name, equations, graph and caption. Original nonstat results remain unchanged and are never merged with it.

It starts only after current-version gates and frozen precision/configuration. This is a fixed-round sequential intervention-sequence comparison. Methods commit different earlier actions, so later-slice values are not optimization of one identical fixed-context function. ## Mandatory gates and reporting rules

- **Information firewall:** real, poisoned and disabled scorers produce identical learner state and next decisions. Test actual pinned CEO, including its acquisition fallback. Initial recommendations, acquisition baselines and all outcome bookkeeping must use purchased measurements only.
- **Environment and manifest parity:** common initial-arm rows and observational hashes match across conditions; deleting/reordering unrelated arms or adding fit/scoring RNG calls cannot change them. Every purchase yields exactly one full SCM row. Analytical means agree with independent simulation at boundaries and ordinary points.
- **Budget/event ledger:** charge initial, sequential and split rows once; no free new-arm data or overshoot; zero and negative intervention values are valid clamps. Recommendation changes become visible immediately after the measurement that permits them. No invented pre-initialization incumbent.
- **Noise and scalar equivalence:** exact and noisy likelihood policies are separate, executable and logged; corrected singleton MCBO/QMCBO posteriors/gradients match under the same policy. Noisy observations must not accidentally enter a fixed near-zero likelihood.
- **Protected contracts and attribution:** compare complete trajectories, not only final rounded values; explicitly test fallback mediation before using “prior only.” No performance ordering is a pass condition. The PP/MC structural floors hold for the externally scored eligible recommendations up to 1e-8 numerical tolerance.
- **Model-based semantics:** physical actions agree with environment mapping, eta coordinates are unchanged, constant features stay finite, identical acquisition calls with fixed base noise have identical values/gradients, and score precision cannot perturb learner history.
- **Provenance/resume:** completion requires matching source/dependency/patch/config/data hashes and an intact event ledger. Row count or sidecar existence alone is insufficient. Never overwrite historical artifacts or mix versions silently.

Use paired replicate-level contrasts and two-sided 95% intervals; report individual seed outcomes, medians, costs, runtime and failures. A seed, not an arm/trial/cost checkpoint, is the independent replication unit. Do not pool duplicate or aliased trajectories. Report every prespecified method and contrast, including null or reversed effects.

Numerical/contract failures stop the affected block. Fix a demonstrated bug, version the patch, and rerun every affected paired unit; do not rerun only poor outcomes or discard failing seeds. Set wall-time limits from engineering timings before final launch; mark timeouts and missing results explicitly. No sample-size extension, benchmark change, likelihood retuning or threshold change may be selected from final rankings. Any necessary protocol amendment must explain its cause and identify already observed runs as exploratory under the prior version.

The final manuscript must distinguish: analytically valid mechanism examples; historical executions with limited interpretation; newly validated corrected comparisons; and still-pending comparisons. Passing software checks alone is not proof of empirical superiority, and a positive performance gap does not establish a universal coarsening advantage.
